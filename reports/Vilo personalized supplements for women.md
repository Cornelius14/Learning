# Personalize Vilo by life stage, not cycle

Vilo can pull almost any signal a woman generates into its app, but only five inputs reliably change what goes into her supplement: **life stage, medications and conditions, menstrual blood loss and diet pattern** (all collectable in an 8 to 12 minute onboarding), plus **a physician-ordered ferritin and iron panel** that decides whether she gets 0, 18 or 60 mg of iron. Wearables, CGMs, photo food logs, DTC genetics and microbiome kits belong in the product as engagement and outcome tracking, not as dosing inputs: no study ties them to dose changes, cycle-phase dosing has essentially no evidence, and no trial shows a personalized formula beats a good multivitamin. The defensible engine is therefore a deterministic, versioned rule set ratified by a clinical board: about six life-stage base blends, lab-driven adjustments for iron, B12, omega-3 and vitamin D, upper-limit accounting on total intake, drug-interaction lockouts, and human review of every flagged case. Buy finished per-customer packs from OK Capsule or Persona's white-label service until roughly 2,000 to 5,000 subscribers (about $35 to $45 landed per customer-month, est.), then move to a base-plus-add-ons hybrid and own pouching past 10,000 (about $17 to $35, est.). The largest legal exposures are ad pixels that leak health or cycle data (Meta lost a 2025 jury verdict over Flo data under a California wiretap law carrying $5,000 per violation), disease claims about PCOS, fertility or anemia, and a patient-facing engine that turns lab values into treatment doses, which is why a physician network should order and review every lab. The economics sit in a narrow band: the research base case ($69 a month, $150 acquisition cost per subscriber, 7% steady monthly churn) returns about 3.0x LTV:CAC with a 5-month payback, but at outsourced MVP pack costs ($35 to $44 a month, est.) the same case drops to roughly 1.4x to 1.8x by my extrapolation, so launch the lab tier near $89 a month or annual prepay and keep paid acquisition small until hybrid manufacturing lands. The competitors that matter are not quiz-pack brands (Care/of is gone) but Hims & Hers, Oura, Function and Superpower, which already own testing and millions of members and could add formulated supplements quickly.

*How to read the labels in this report.* Every research track hit network fetch blocks and a shared search-budget cap, so most cited facts were confirmed from search-result summaries of the linked pages rather than full reads. A plain link means the fact appeared in that source. **(est.)** marks an estimate made by the research team or by me from cited inputs; none are vendor quotes. **(unverified)** marks a figure that is single-sourced, conflicts across sources, or comes from a vendor or competitor marketing page. **[NV]** marks clinical reference values (mostly NIH ODS and NASEM figures) that the researchers cited to canonical sources but could not re-check live. Proposed dosing and referral rules are the research team's synthesis, not guideline text. Everything tagged this way is collected in the verify list near the end.

## Nine popular assumptions the evidence rejects

The pitch for personalized women's supplements leans on ideas that sound scientific and fail on inspection. Getting them wrong costs Vilo twice: it spends engineering on inputs that never change the formula, and it invites exactly the claims that FTC and FDA enforcement targets. The table states each assumption, what the research found, and what to build instead.

| Common assumption | What the research found | What Vilo should do instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Cycle syncing:** change nutrients between follicular and luteal phases | A 2023 *Journal of Applied Physiology* review found "little to no evidence" for cycle syncing of diet, exercise or supplements ([UPI/HealthDay](https://www.upi.com/Health_News/2023/12/06/women-exercise-menstrual-cycle/2311701875870/)). Retail phase-by-phase supplement lists cite no studies. PMS trials that worked used continuous daily dosing ([PMC11723155](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11723155/)) | Dose daily. Use cycle data to time symptom check-ins, size iron need from bleeding, and flag possible pregnancy |
| **Wearables should set doses** (HRV, sleep, temperature) | No study or society guideline uses wearable metrics as a dosing input. The best magnesium RCT improved Oura-measured heart rate and HRV but **not objective sleep** ([PMC12832366](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12832366/)) | Use wearables to show outcomes and as a pregnancy guardrail; never auto-titrate |
| **A personalized formula beats a standard multivitamin** | No RCT shows it. In Food4Me (1,269 completers, 7 countries), adding phenotype or genotype data gave "no evidence" of benefit beyond diet-based advice ([PubMed 27524815](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27524815/)). ZOE's positive trial gave the personalized arm far more app engagement than controls ([FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2024/05/10/Zoe-hails-personalized-nutrition-trial-success-results-come-under-scrutiny/)) | Claim the process, not superior outcomes, and fund your own trial against a good multi |
| **More biomarkers means better personalization** | Sex hormones, AMH, cortisol, RBC magnesium and zinc do not reliably change a formula; ferritin, B12, the omega-3 index and, modestly, vitamin D do | Run a lean, safety-gated panel; send diagnostic questions to clinicians |
| **Every woman should test vitamin D** | The Endocrine Society's 2024 guideline advises **against** routine 25(OH)D testing and against above-DRI doses in healthy adults under 75 ([PubMed 38828931](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828931/)) | Default to 600 to 1,000 IU D3; test only inside the broader panel; never market a "target level" |
| **MTHFR status should pick the folate form** | ACMG advises against MTHFR genotyping, and genotype does not change folic acid advice ([PubMed 23288205](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23288205/)) | 400 mcg folic acid for anyone who could become pregnant; methylfolate as an option, never a genotype rule |
| **Daily food logging gives micronutrient precision** | Fewer than half of trial participants still log after week 10 ([PubMed 31155473](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31155473/)); only 2.58% of a free photo app's users were active ([JMIR 2014](https://www.jmir.org/2014/4/e109/)); no study validates photo-based micronutrient estimates | A 15 to 25 item screener refreshed quarterly, plus an optional 3-day photo snapshot |
| **Gummies or one custom-blended pill is the ideal format** | Gummies rarely carry iron and are poor mineral carriers; CSPI found none of 37 gummy multis a good substitute for a tablet ([CSPI](https://www.cspinet.org/news/dont-rely-gummy-multivitamin-if-you-can-swallow-tablet-20171121)). The research found no US contract maker offering true per-person compounding | Capsules, tablets and softgels in daily pouches, plus a sachet for gram-dose actives |
| **Supplements become HSA/FSA-eligible with a doctor's letter** | The IRS warned in 2024 that questionnaire-based letters don't make supplements eligible; they qualify only "rarely" ([Kiplinger](https://kiplinger.com/taxes/hsa-expenses-when-a-doctors-note-isnt-enough)) | Make the physician-ordered lab line HSA-friendly; never advertise the supplement as eligible |

The pattern behind the table is the core strategic insight of this report. The evidence-backed form of personalization is narrow: correct documented deficiencies with lab-guided doses, fit the formula to life stage, and prevent harm from upper limits and drug interactions. That narrow version is cheap to collect, simple to manufacture and auditable by regulators. Everything beyond it is engagement, which is valuable for retention but must never be sold as efficacy.

## Four onboarding answers outweigh every wearable stream

### What to collect, ranked by how much it changes the formula

The ranking below is the research team's synthesis of cited evidence and standard nutrition practice, not a published framework. Labs are covered in the next section and would sit near the top.

| Rank | Signal | Exactly how Vilo collects it | What it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Life stage:** cycling, contraception type, trying to conceive, pregnant (trimester), postpartum, breastfeeding, perimenopause, postmenopause, on MHT | Onboarding question, monthly "anything changed?" prompt, HealthKit `contraceptive` and `lactation` records ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategorytypeidentifier.md)) | Picks the base blend and safety limits (folate, iodine, choline, iron, DHA, vitamin A form) |
| 2 | **Medications and conditions** | Free NLM RxTerms/RxNorm autocomplete ([RxNav](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/RxNav/TermsofService.html)) plus checkboxes for about 12 drug classes and a condition checklist | Lockouts, B12 additions, timing splits |
| 3 | **Menstrual blood loss** | SAMANTA-style heavy-bleeding screen (86.7% sensitivity, 89.5% specificity, [PMC12547405](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12547405/)) plus HealthKit `menstrualFlow` and Health Connect menstruation records | Strongest non-lab iron proxy |
| 4 | **Diet pattern** | 15 to 25 items built from the free 19-item NCI Dietary Screener plus iron, calcium, vitamin D, fish, iodized salt, egg and vegan items, refreshed quarterly | B12, iron, omega-3, calcium, iodine, choline; feeds upper-limit math |
| 5 | Vitamin D context | Sun exposure, skin tone, season from coarse location, weight from self-report or a synced scale | Vitamin D dose |
| 6 | Symptom clusters | Menopause Rating Scale for women 40+, luteal-phase PMS log for opt-ins, PHQ-2/GAD-2 for safety routing only | Turns on PMS, migraine or menopause modules; tracks response |
| 7 | Temperature and cycle phase | HealthKit `appleSleepingWristTemperature`, Health Connect skin temperature, Oura `temperature_deviation` | Pregnancy guardrail and anovulation flag; no dose changes |
| 8 | Activity | Steps and workouts from HealthKit or Health Connect | Endurance-athlete iron flag |
| 9 | Sleep, HRV, resting heart rate | Same, passively | Outcome tracking only |
| 10 | CGM glucose | Abbott Lingo via Apple Health; Dexcom Stelo only through approved partners | Little; optional PCOS context |
| 11 | Photo food logs | Passio or FatSecret, optional quarterly snapshot | Little for micronutrients |
| 12 | DTC genetics | Optional 23andMe or Ancestry raw-file upload, parse HFE only, delete the file | HFE variant becomes a no-iron flag |
| 13 | DTC microbiome | Do not integrate | Nothing |

### Read phone health stores natively; add vendor APIs only for gaps

The base layer costs nothing. Vilo's native iOS and Android apps read Apple HealthKit and Android Health Connect on the device. HealthKit exposes the women's-health records that matter, including `menstrualFlow`, `ovulationTestResult`, `contraceptive` and `lactation` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategorytypeidentifier.md)). Apple Watch Series 8 or later, all Ultra models and SE 3 sample wrist temperature every 5 seconds during sleep after a roughly 5-night baseline ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT213275)), and record nothing if she doesn't wear the watch to bed ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/764078)). Health Connect carries skin temperature and cycle-tracking records, but **by default an app sees only 30 days of history before its first permission grant**, so request `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY` at onboarding or lose the backfill ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/data-types)). Because Oura, Withings and many other devices write into these stores, native reads cover most users with no vendor contract.

Direct vendor APIs are unstable in late 2026, which is the best argument for keeping them optional. **Garmin paused all new developer approvals in spring 2026** and had given no reopening date by mid-September ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/14/garmin-developer-api-access-paused/)). The legacy Fitbit Web API shuts off around Oct 30 to Nov 2, 2026, and every user must re-consent on the new Google Health API ([Google](https://developers.google.com/health/about); [Fitbit community](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Web-API-Development/Introducing-the-next-phase-of-the-Fitbit-Web-API/m-p/5828051)). Oura caps unapproved apps at 10 users, exposes temperature only as deviation from baseline, and cuts API access for Gen3 and Ring 4 owners whose membership lapses ([Oura support](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/4415266939155-The-Oura-API); [Oura partner support](https://partnersupport.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/20949682312211-The-Oura-API)); its cycle predictions stay in-app, per a community feature request ([Oura community](https://oura-community.canny.io/feature-requests/p/mensuration-ovulation-data-added-to-oura-api)). WHOOP defaults to 100 requests a minute and 10,000 a day ([WHOOP](https://developer.whoop.com/docs/developing/rate-limiting)), and developers report long waits to lift its 10-user cap ([WHOOP community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/no-response-on-api-app-approval-to-raise-the-10-user-limit/15246.md?page=2)). Withings requires its paid Withings+ subscription on the free API plan from Oct 12, 2026 ([Withings](https://developer.withings.com/llms.md)), Samsung retired its legacy SDK in July 2025 ([Samsung](https://developer.samsung.com/health/android/migration-guide/overview.html)), and Eight Sleep has no official API ([API Evangelist](https://providers.apievangelist.com/providers/eight-sleep/)). Oura's API agreement also restricts storing, aggregating or transferring its data beyond what is expressly permitted ([Oura API Agreement](https://cloud.ouraring.com/legal/api-agreement)), which matters if Vilo ever pools data for research or partners.

If users demand ring and strap data, buy rather than build. Terra lists plans from $399 a month ([Terra](https://www.tryterra.co/)), and its credit pricing works out to about **$1 per connected user per month** (est., from [Terra docs](https://docs.tryterra.co/health-and-fitness-api/pricing)); Thryve charges €499 a month for 500 users ([Thryve](https://www.thryve.health/pricing)); Spike starts at $450 a month ([Spike](https://spikeapi.com/pricing)); Junction's own page shows a $300-a-month Launch plan while a competitor says no pricing is published (unverified) ([Junction](https://www.junction.com/pricing); [Sahha](https://sahha.ai/compare/junction-alternatives/)). A formula that changes monthly needs only daily or weekly batch pulls, so streaming add-ons are wasted money. **Recommendation: ship HealthKit and Health Connect only at MVP**, and add an aggregator in Phase 2 only if cohort data show wearable users retain better.

### Cycle data earns its place through life stage, iron and pregnancy safety

Wearable temperature reliably confirms ovulation after the fact. Oura's company-authored validation detected 96.4% of ovulations with a ±1.26-day error, against ±3.44 days for the calendar method, across 1,155 cycles ([PMC11829181](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11829181/)); Apple's study, still a preprint, reported a 1.22-day mean error ([JMIR Preprints](https://preprints.jmir.org/preprint/50821)). That accuracy has regulatory teeth: Natural Cycles needed FDA clearance to use Apple Watch temperature for contraception ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/natural-cycles-receives-fda-clearance-to-integrate-its-birth-control-app-with-data-measured-by-apple-watch)), so **Vilo must never present a fertile window for conception or contraception**. Its legitimate uses are three. First, life-stage hints: several cycles without a temperature shift, or very irregular cycles, trigger a "talk to a clinician" prompt rather than a formula change. Second, a pregnancy guardrail: a sustained temperature rise plus a missed period switches her to the prenatal-safe formula until a test says otherwise. Third, iron: heavy menstrual bleeding doubled iron-deficiency prevalence in a Swedish teen cohort (52% versus 26%) ([Lund University](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/article/heavy-menstruation-common-among-teenage-girls-questionnaire-reveals-risk-iron-deficiency)), and more than two heavy days was linked to ferritin below 30 µg/L in young Australian women (AOR 1.73) ([Monash](https://www.monash.edu/medicine/sphpm/womenshealth/research/findings/iron-insufficiency-among-young-australian-women)). Urine hormone monitors such as Mira and Inito offer no public developer API; read whatever their apps write to HealthKit `ovulationTestResult`, accept manual entry or exported reports, and treat a richer feed as a partnership project ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/02/10/mira-oura-partnership-hormone-tracking-integration/); [Inito](https://inito.com)).

### Diet: screen quarterly instead of logging daily

Daily diet tracking is the most common design mistake in this category. Logging falls from about 6 days a week to 2 by week 8 in app interventions ([NCT06311175 protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/75/NCT06311175/Prot_000.pdf)), and the AI photo studies that exist measure calories and macronutrients, with protein and fat the weakest ([MDPI Nutrients 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/6/966/htm); [PMC12513282](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12513282/)); none validates iron, folate, vitamin D or B12 from photos. What the formula needs is pattern data: vegan or vegetarian, dairy and fortified foods, fatty fish per week, red meat and legumes, eggs, iodized salt. Build a 15 to 25 item composite from the free **NCI Dietary Screener Questionnaire**, whose estimates agreed closely with two 24-hour recalls in NHANES ([NCI](https://epi.grants.cancer.gov/nhanes/dietscreen/evaluation.html)), plus validated targeted items such as the IRONIC-FFQ for iron ([PMC5372862](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5372862/)), and disclose that the combined screener is itself unvalidated. If you want photo logging for engagement, Passio's token pricing implies about $0.63 to $6.25 per active photo-logging user per month depending on tier (my arithmetic from [Passio](https://www.passio.ai/platform) and its 250,000-tokens-per-active-user estimate in [Passio docs](https://passio.gitbook.io/nutrition-ai/getting-started/token-system-and-pricing)); FatSecret's basic tier is free including commercial use ([FatSecret](https://platform.fatsecret.com/)).

### Medications, questionnaires, vitals and the sources to skip

Medications are the highest-value, lowest-friction input because they act as safety constraints. NLM's RxNorm APIs are free, need no license for normal use, and allow 20 requests per second ([RxNav terms](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/RxNav/TermsofService.html)); pair autocomplete with checkboxes so users don't skip hormonal contraception, thyroid hormone, SSRIs and SNRIs, metformin, GLP-1 drugs, PPIs, anticoagulants, MHT, antiepileptics, isotretinoin, spironolactone and lithium. For interaction content, a pharmacist-curated rule set covering about 20 drug classes is cheaper and more auditable than DrugBank's commercial license, whose prices are not public ([DrugBank](https://go.drugbank.com/pricing/)). Questionnaire licensing varies widely. PHQ-9 and GAD-7 need no permission ([ND HHS](https://www.hhs.nd.gov/sites/www/files/documents/BH/BHC/PHQ-9-and-GAD-7.pdf)), but PHQ-9 item 9 asks about suicidal thoughts, so it must route to crisis resources and never drive formulation. The Menopause Rating Scale was usable "without formal permission" per its 2003 authors ([PMC183844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC183844/)); the Greene scale is licensed through RWS ([RWS](https://lifesciences.rws.com/greeneclimactericscale/faqs)); the PSQI sleep index costs $1,000 a year and up for commercial use ([Pitt](https://inventions.pitt.edu/technologies/pittsburgh-sleep-quality-index--02140)); the PSST PMS screen is licensed through McMaster ([McMaster](https://research.mcmaster.ca/industry-investors/tech/quality-of-life-questionnaires-tools/copyright-04-069/)).

Vitals matter as context and safety, not as optimization levers. Body weight adjusts vitamin D because BMI predicts the response to a dose and obese participants averaged 19.8 nmol/L lower 25(OH)D ([PMC4220998](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4220998/)); blood pressure from Withings' Public API ([Withings](https://developer.withings.com/llms.md)) or Apple Health becomes a caution flag for stimulant botanicals. Skip the rest. Microbiome kits failed a 2026 reproducibility study in which identical stool sent to seven DTC companies produced between-company differences as large as the differences between people ([University of Maryland](https://www.umaryland.edu/news/archived-news/march-2026/gut-check-study-raises-questions-about-at-home-microbiome-testing.php)). CGMs cost users about $89 a month for Stelo ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-prices-first-over-the-counter-glucose-monitor/725310/)), the evidence for non-diabetic use is limited ([Eur J Med Res 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40001-026-03920-0)), Lingo writes to Apple Health only with a 3-hour delay ([Lingo](https://support.hellolingo.com/hc/en-us/articles/27954887462290-How-do-I-connect-Lingo-to-Apple-Health)), and Dexcom caps limited-access production apps at 5 users ([Dexcom](https://developer.dexcom.com/docs/dexcom/scopes-access)). Genetics adds one useful flag, HFE hemochromatosis variants as an iron lockout; 23andMe still offers raw-data download under its nonprofit owner TTAM ([23andMe](https://customercare.23andme.com/hc/en-us/articles/212196868); [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/23andme-officially-sold-happen-customers-194104258.html)), so parse that flag, store only the result, and delete the file.

### A collection cadence built for real adherence

Design for bursts, not streams. **Onboarding takes 8 to 12 minutes**: life stage, medications and conditions, the bleeding screen, the diet screener, and one or two symptom scales chosen by life stage, followed by a one-tap HealthKit or Health Connect connection with history permission. **Before every shipment, a 60 to 90 second check-in** asks whether stage, medications or bleeding changed and collects three to five symptom sliders; tying it to "confirm next month's formula" makes the subscription itself the compliance hook (a design hypothesis, not a sourced finding). Offer an optional 3-day photo snapshot each quarter, and daily symptom logging only to women who opt into PMS tracking, since a DRSP-based PMDD assessment needs two full cycles of daily entries ([IAPMD](https://www.iapmd.org/symptom-tracker)). Assume wearable features reach a wealthier subset; the questionnaire path must stand alone.

## Ferritin is the blood test that earns its cost

### Six results change the formula; most of a longevity panel does not

The 2026 American Society of Hematology guideline is the most important single fact for Vilo's lab tier. It raised the ferritin cutoff for iron deficiency to **30 ng/mL or below** for menstruating and pregnant adults, rejected the old 15 ng/mL cutoff, and allows up to 50 ng/mL for selected high-risk groups such as heavy menstrual bleeding ([US Pharmacist](https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/ash-guidelines-raise-ferritin-thresholds-for-iron-deficiency/)), citing a pooled **27% global prevalence** of iron deficiency ([Contemporary OB/GYN](https://www.contemporaryobgyn.net/view/faq-ash-iron-deficiency-ferritin-thresholds-diagnosis)). Iron is also the nutrient whose right dose spans zero (iron-replete, postmenopausal, hemochromatosis) to 60 mg on alternate days, so ferritin moves the formula more than any other result. Vitamin D runs the other way: the Endocrine Society found no evidence-based target levels and advises against routine testing ([PubMed 38828931](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828931/); [AAFP 2026](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2026/0300/practice-guidelines-vitamin-d-disease-prevention.html)), so a vitamin D result changes the dose only modestly and invites clinician pushback.

The per-marker judgments below come from the lab research track; several rest on the researcher's clinical background rather than sources fetched in-session, so the clinical board should confirm them.

| Marker | Changes the formula? | Collection note |
|---|---|---|
| Ferritin with iron, TIBC, TSAT and hsCRP | **Yes, strongly:** iron in or out, and dose | Venous; hsCRP needed because inflammation raises ferritin |
| B12 with MMA reflex | **Yes:** dose for vegans, metformin and PPI users | Biotin and high-dose B12 distort results |
| Omega-3 index | **Yes:** EPA/DHA dose toward the >8% target | Dried blood spot correlates well with RBC measures ([AIS](https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements/group_b/other/fish-oils/determining-your-omega-3-index)) |
| 25(OH)D | Modestly | Guideline against routine testing |
| RBC folate | Only for conception planning | Folic acid 400 mcg is the default regardless |
| CBC, CMP, TSH with reflex FT4, HbA1c, lipids | **Safety gates and referral triggers** | CMP calcium and eGFR bound vitamin D and magnesium; biotin skews thyroid assays |
| Estradiol, progesterone, LH, FSH, AMH, cortisol, prolactin, RBC magnesium, zinc | **No** | Diagnostic or confounded; route to clinicians |

Recommended core panel for every lab-tier user: CBC, ferritin with iron studies, hsCRP, 25(OH)D, B12 with MMA reflex, folate, TSH with reflex FT4, HbA1c, lipids and CMP, plus an omega-3 index dried-blood-spot kit, which routine Quest and Labcorp panels reportedly don't carry (unverified). Life-stage add-ons: RBC folate and TPO antibodies when trying to conceive, ApoB in peri- and postmenopause, and a PCOS track (testosterone, SHBG, DHEA-S, fasting insulin) only with clinician involvement. In postmenopausal women testing mostly stops unneeded iron, and a low ferritin there should trigger a GI-bleed referral, not a supplement.

### Venous draws for the core panel, dried blood spots for omega-3

Only a venous draw at a Quest or Labcorp patient service center (PSC), or by a mobile phlebotomist, reliably supports iron studies, CBC, CMP, folate and B12 together. Capillary data are promising but uneven: one 2026 device study found ferritin r = 0.998 against venepuncture ([Becaris 2026](https://becarispublishing.com/doi/10.57264/jpc-2026-0009)), yet a trial comparing capillary and venous vitamin D has posted no results ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07782528)) and SiPhox's home device is not FDA-cleared ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/OKm-siphox-health-painless-at-home-thyroid-testing)). Self-collection mostly works but not cleanly: 95% of SIREN participants got a sample and 17% reported problems ([PMC12477433](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12477433/)). The structural change of 2026 is that **Function Health bought Getlabs on April 8, 2026** ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/function-health-acquires-mobile-healthcare-platform-getlabs-provide-members-home-lab)), so the leading independent mobile-phlebotomy network now belongs to a direct competitor (Crunchbase still lists the deal as pending, unverified). Getlabs still dispatches by API and lets partners subsidize or pass through the fee ([Getlabs](https://getlabs.com/api)), which has ranged up to $90 in one practitioner program ([Genova](https://gdx.net/getlabs/about)). Default to PSC draws, offer paid mobile draws as a convenience, and use dried blood spots only for the omega-3 index and convenience retests.

### A physician network must order, review and own critical values

Junction is the most turnkey stack for a consumer app: physician ordering, Quest, Labcorp and BioReference requisitions, at-home phlebotomy and webhooked results. Lab testing sits in its custom-priced Enterprise tier ([Sahha](https://sahha.ai/compare/junction-alternatives/)), with a flat collection fee per walk-in order and example reflex-test prices of $17.50 to $21.50 ([Junction docs](https://docs.junction.com/lab/overview/order-pricing)). Health Gorilla is the FHIR-native alternative, but ordering providers need their own Quest and Labcorp account numbers ([Medplum](https://www.medplum.com/docs/integration/health-gorilla)); it partners with Wheel's clinician network ([Health Gorilla](https://www.healthgorilla.com/blog/health-gorilla-partners-with-wheel-to-extend-diagnostic-ordering-through-wheels-virtual-care-ecosystem)). Rupa Health's ordering has closed and moved into Fullscript, a practitioner storefront rather than an app API ([Rupa](https://www.rupahealth.com/updates/moving-from-rupa-to-labs-on-fullscript)). Physician-network pricing is opaque: one reviewed OpenLoop proposal quoted **$9,000 to implement plus $1,500 a month**, from a competitor-authored page ([Cuvo](https://cuvo.co/compare/cuvo-vs-openloop)), and two smaller networks advertise **$25 per completed consult** ([Cuvo](https://cuvo.co/blog/best-telehealth-provider-networks)), both unverified. Contract a 50-state network that orders under an asynchronous intake protocol, reviews every result, covers critical-value callbacks around the clock, and signs off on any lab-driven change above routine thresholds, inside a management services organization and physician-owned professional corporation structure to respect corporate-practice-of-medicine rules (counsel to confirm). Patients can get reports directly from CLIA labs ([ACP on CMS-2319-F](https://www.acponline.org/practice-career/regulatory-resources/regulatory-compliance/summary-of-clia-programs-and-hipaa-privacy-rule-patients-access-to-test-reports-cms-2319-f-final)), so don't try to gatekeep results.

State lines shape the launch map. Reseller lists say Labcorp won't fill direct-access orders in NY, NJ, RI, MA and MD, and Quest won't in NY, NJ, RI and AZ ([Walk-In Lab](https://www.walkinlab.com/help-contents); [Own Your Labs](https://ownyourlabs.com/faq/)), which is unverified against the labs themselves. Superpower serves NY and NJ at $399 a year "where state fees apply" but not Rhode Island ([Superpower FAQ](https://superpower.com/faqs)), and Junction routed NY and NJ patients to BioReference ([Junction](https://www.junction.com/post/lab-testing-new-york-new-jersey)). **Launch the lab tier where network-physician orders work, exclude Rhode Island, and either route NY and NJ through BioReference at higher cost or defer them.** The 2024 FDA rule treating lab-developed tests as devices was vacated in March 2025 ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/04/federal-district-court-vacates-fdas-ldt-final-rule)), so CLIA and state licensure attach to the performing labs, not to Vilo's app.

### Results need LOINC coding and a human confirmation step

Native orders should arrive as vendor webhooks or FHIR Observations keyed by LOINC, with the original PDF, performing lab, units and reference range stored alongside. For outside results, iOS users can import Apple Health Records, which requires providers' FHIR R4 US Core endpoints ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/healthregister/apd12d144779/web)), and portal data can come through FHIR aggregators such as 1upHealth's approved apps including Flexpa ([1upHealth](https://docs.1up.health/help-center/Content/en-US/get-started/cms-patient-access/third-party-apps.html)). PDF uploads are the weak link: a vendor benchmark scored LLM lab-report parsing at **0.836 for GPT-4o and 0.879 for Claude 3.5 Sonnet** against 0.942 for its own model ([Eka Care](https://developer.eka.care/eka-medai/technologies/lab-report)), too error-prone to dose iron or vitamin D unattended, especially with unit traps such as ng/mL versus nmol/L for vitamin D. Parse, normalize units, run plausibility checks, make the user confirm each value, and require clinician review before any uploaded value changes a dose.

### Retest at three to four months and plan for 50 to 75% completion

The omega-3 index needs about four months of steady intake to settle ([FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2019/01/25/How-much-EPA-DHA-omega-3-do-you-need-for-a-desirable-Omega-3-Index-score)), and ferritin, vitamin D and B12 rechecks at 8 to 12 weeks are standard practice (background knowledge, unverified), so a baseline, a targeted month-3 to month-4 retest (ferritin, vitamin D, omega-3 index) and an annual full panel fit the biology. No consumer-health company publishes order-to-draw completion. Adjacent evidence says unreturned kits, not failed collection, are the main loss ([PMC7975241](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7975241/)), and better reminders lifted mailed stool-kit returns from 61.5% to 71.9% ([PMC9206862](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9206862/)). **Plan for 50 to 75% of lab-tier buyers to complete a draw (est.)**, and always ship a questionnaire-based formula so non-drawers still get a product. Lab fees that are part of medical care are generally HSA/FSA-eligible, and Labcorp OnDemand markets wellness tests as eligible ([Labcorp OnDemand](https://www.ondemand.labcorp.com/hsa-fsa)), so itemize the physician-ordered panel as its own line.

## A versioned rules engine with hard stops beats an AI dosing model

### What the evidence supports, and what it does not

The strong evidence clusters around a short list of nutrients. For iron, alternate-day dosing raised cumulative fractional absorption to **21.8% from 16.3%** with consecutive days in iron-depleted women ([Stoffel 2017](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/handle/20.500.11850/233029?show=full)), and a 150-woman RCT found equal ferritin gains with fewer GI side effects ([EClinicalMedicine 2023](https://doaj.org/article/628bd28bfd6645a5820f45e50dda2c27)); ferrous bisglycinate cut GI adverse events by about 64% against other iron salts in a 17-trial meta-analysis ([PubMed 36728680](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36728680/)). In pregnancy, omega-3 cut preterm birth (RR 0.89) and early preterm birth (RR 0.58) across 70 trials, with a suggested 500 to 1,000 mg daily including at least 500 mg DHA ([Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/news/new-research-finds-omega-3-fatty-acids-reduce-risk-premature-birth)), and ODS notes some prenatals contain no iodine and little or no choline ([ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Pregnancy-HealthProfessional/)). B12 monitoring for metformin users carries an ADA Grade B ([PubMed 39651989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39651989/)); B6, calcium and zinc consistently helped psychological PMS symptoms in a 2024 review ([PMC11723155](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11723155/)); magnesium and riboflavin 400 mg have headache-society support for migraine ([AHS](https://americanheadachesociety.org/news/incorporating-nutraceuticals-for-migraine-prevention)); and creatine at 5 g or more with resistance training adds lean mass in postmenopausal women ([PubMed 42141930](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42141930/)).

The weak side matters as much. Myo-inositol for PCOS was judged "limited and inconclusive" in the review behind the 2023 international guideline ([PMC11099481](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11099481/)); The Menopause Society does not recommend supplements or herbal remedies for hot flashes ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/womens-health-ob-gyn/20230607/nams-releases-recommendations-to-treat-menopause-symptoms-with-nonhormone-therapies)); and NIH LiverTox calls black cohosh products a "well established cause of clinically apparent liver injury" ([LiverTox](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547990/)). Supplements reliably move blood levels, but that is a surrogate: a multivitamin raised biomarkers in 55 to 65 year-olds without improving cognition ([PMC4446780](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4446780/)). Vilo's honest promise is correcting documented shortfalls and fitting life stage safely, not optimizing outcomes.

### Upper limits apply to total intake, and Europe sets stricter ones

Every nutrient with a total-intake UL must be summed across diet, Vilo and any other supplement the user declares, with Vilo's dose capped at the UL minus everything else and a working target of **no more than 80% of the UL** (est. buffer). A handful of ULs count only supplements and fortificants: magnesium, folic acid, niacin, vitamin E and preformed vitamin A. Where diet data is missing, assume a high intake for her demographic rather than zero. B6 is the live safety issue: Australia's TGA will move products above 50 mg a day to pharmacist-only status from June 1, 2027 after **250 neuropathy reports** between January 2023 and October 2025 ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/11/25/vitamin-b6-supplements-face-new-regulation-in-australia)), and EFSA set an adult UL of **12 mg** that includes pregnancy ([PMC10189633](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10189633)), against a US UL of 100 mg [NV]. Cap B6 at 25 mg by default even in the US.

### Interactions and timing decide the pack architecture

Two interactions shape the physical product. Levothyroxine binds calcium, iron and magnesium in the gut, so those minerals belong at least 4 hours after the dose ([Patient.info](https://patient.info/medication-interactions/iron-and-levothyroxine-interaction)). Biotin can cause undetected false lab results, including thyroid assays ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/in-vitro-diagnostics/biotin-interference-troponin-lab-tests-assays-subject-biotin-interference)), and ZRT tells users to stop biotin for 3 days before blood-spot tests ([ZRT](https://www.zrtlab.com/media/3381/blood-inx-42826.pdf)), so **fix biotin at about 30 mcg and never sell 5 to 10 mg "hair" biotin**, or Vilo will corrupt its own panels.

| User input | Engine rule |
|---|---|
| Levothyroxine | Every mineral at least 4 hours after the dose: the AM pack and iron move to lunch, calcium, magnesium and zinc stay in the PM pack; no kelp or high iodine |
| Warfarin | Fixed low vitamin K; no St John's wort, ginkgo, dong quai or high-dose curcumin; vitamin E at RDA; fish oil only with clinician sign-off; pharmacist review before shipping ([UCSD](https://health.ucsd.edu/for-health-care-professionals/anticoagulation-guidelines/warfarin/supplement-interactions)) |
| SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans | No St John's wort, 5-HTP, tryptophan or SAMe |
| Hormonal contraception | No St John's wort; optional low B6; no "pill depletion" marketing, since modern low-dose pills don't clearly lower folate ([PubMed 21967158](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21967158/)) |
| Metformin; PPI over 1 year | Add B12; PPI users get calcium citrate and a magnesium check |
| Isotretinoin | No preformed vitamin A |
| GLP-1 drugs | RDA-level multi plus protein and strength-training prompts, per the 2025 joint advisory ([PubMed 40450457](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40450457/)) |
| Spironolactone, ACE inhibitors, ARBs | No potassium |
| Antiepileptics, lithium, transplant drugs, chemotherapy | Route to pharmacist or physician; no botanicals |

The pack follows from these rules: an **AM pack** with breakfast (B vitamins with low B6, folate, B12, iodine, D3, vitamin C, omega-3 softgel); **iron as a separately marked unit on alternate mornings**, away from coffee, tea and dairy; and a **PM pack** with dinner (calcium in doses of 500 mg or less [NV], magnesium, zinc with copper).

### Forms and capsule physics cap what a daily pack can hold

Form choices with real evidence are ferrous bisglycinate, D3 over D2 [NV], folic acid as the preconception form, cyanocobalamin [NV], magnesium glycinate or citrate rather than poorly absorbed oxide ([PubMed 10746516](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10746516/)), calcium citrate for PPI users [NV], potassium iodide, beta-carotene rather than retinol for women of reproductive age, algal DHA for vegans, and creatine monohydrate. Physics then binds. By the formulation researcher's stoichiometry, 300 mg of elemental magnesium takes about 2.1 g of pure bisglycinate and 500 mg of calcium about 2.4 g of citrate, while a size 00 capsule holds roughly 500 to 800 mg of powder [NV]. Full-dose magnesium plus calcium alone therefore needs 5 to 7 capsules. The practical daily pack is **1 to 2 core capsules, 0 to 4 tiered mineral or specialty capsules, an omega-3 softgel, and an optional sachet** for gram-dose actives such as creatine (3 to 5 g), myo-inositol (2 to 4 g) or collagen.

### The engine pipeline: rules first, humans on flags, machine learning at the edges

Build the engine as deterministic, versioned rules in which every rule cites its source and every formula is reproducible. It runs in eight steps. It normalizes inputs (units, collection dates, staleness rules such as ferritin older than six months, and confounders like high CRP or recent biotin). It evaluates the hard-stop and referral layer first. It picks the life-stage base, applies lab adjustments, and adds symptom modules with time-boxed reassessment. A constraint solver then sums intake against ULs, applies interactions and pack placement, and drops the lowest-evidence item when capsule capacity runs out. Flagged cases go to a human queue, and retests are scheduled.

Proposed referral thresholds, which the board must set because they come from standard practice rather than one retrieved guideline, include hemoglobin below 12 g/dL, ferritin above 200 ng/mL with TSAT above 45%, TSH outside the lab range, 25(OH)D above 100 ng/mL, eGFR below 60, liver enzymes above 2 to 3 times normal, HbA1c of 6.5% or higher, a positive pregnancy, and use of anticoagulants, lithium, transplant drugs or chemotherapy. **Any flag, watchlist drug, pregnancy, or dose above a routine tier (iron above 45 mg, vitamin D above 2,000 IU, B6 above 25 mg) goes to an RD or PharmD**, with an MD for referrals. Keep machine learning out of the safety layer: it belongs upstream (diet-intake estimation, adherence prediction) or inside fixed bounds (learning an individual's vitamin D response by BMI and baseline). Bioniq's claim of an algorithm trained on more than 40,000 people has no published validation ([AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/meet-the-founder-nutrition-gets-personal-for-bioniqs-vadim-fedotov)); a transparent rule set is what a regulator, a clinician or an FTC investigator can audit.

An ingredient library of **about 35 actives at 2 to 3 dose tiers** covers the evidence: six life-stage base blends (reproductive and not trying, trying to conceive and first trimester, second and third trimester, lactation, perimenopause, postmenopause) with vegan and levothyroxine variants, plus 10 to 15 tiered add-ons (iron 4 tiers, magnesium 4, calcium 3, vitamin D 2 to 3, B12 3, omega-3 3, zinc 3, and on/off specialty modules). That yields thousands of combinations from about 35 inventory SKUs (est.). Botanicals such as black cohosh, vitex, ashwagandha, curcumin and green tea extract stay off by default and clinician-gated.

## Buy finished packs now, own pouching past 10,000 subscribers

### Stock-unit pick-and-pack is the only proven US model

Only one way of making personalized supplements is proven at US scale: stock capsules, tablets and softgels at fixed doses assembled into a customer's daily pouches, as Care/of and Persona did ([Suggestic](https://blog.suggestic.com/top-16-personalized-supplement-brands); [Persona](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/persona-nutrition-launches-white-labeling-service-for-brands-looking-to-expand-into-the-personalized-nutrition-subscription-market-302294913.html)). True per-person compounding lives in proprietary European plants. Bioniq makes granules in Switzerland and assembles formulas in the UK ([Bioniq](https://www.bioniq.com/)) from a fixed set of about 40 granule formulas, which is really pick-and-pack with granules. Rootine reportedly paused its micro-beads in February 2025 (unverified, single source) ([Suggestic](https://blog.suggestic.com/top-16-personalized-supplement-brands)). Nourished prints 500,000 gummies a day in Birmingham and raised £14 million to enter the US, yet paused US orders over import rules ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/healthcare/articles/personalised-gummies-firm-rem3dy-health-140557970.html)). The research found no US contract manufacturer offering per-person compounding, so Vilo's choices are pure pick-and-pack or a hybrid: a standardized life-stage base plus 2 to 5 add-on SKUs.

Here the science and the factory point the same way. Life stage drives most of the evidence-backed formula, and a base-plus-add-ons pack is also the cheapest to make and verify, because every component is a released, tested finished unit and the per-pack check is a count-and-identity vision inspection rather than a per-person batch.

### What one customer-month costs

OK Capsule produces per-customer packets on demand with a 24-hour turnaround and no minimums, charging a packaging fee "starting at $21 per unit" plus contents, and shipping "starting at $7.50," with fees falling at volume ([OK Capsule](https://okcapsule.squarespace.com/api-instructions)). Persona launched a turnkey white-label service in November 2024 that bundles its assessment, algorithm, drug-nutrient interaction checker and fulfillment, with no public pricing ([PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/persona-nutrition-launches-white-labeling-service-for-brands-looking-to-expand-into-the-personalized-nutrition-subscription-market-302294913.html)). Raw nutrients are cheap: vitamin D3 powder runs about $49 to $61 a kg ([PureBulk](https://purebulk.com/products/cholecalciferol-vitamin-d3-bulk)), magnesium glycinate about $9 to $35 a kg ([BulkSupplements](https://www.bulksupplements.com/products/magnesium-glycinate-powder)), and triple-strength fish oil softgels about $0.068 each ([Jedwards](https://bulknaturaloils.com/fish-oil-capsules-triple-strength-1000mg-fg5127.html)). Unit count and assembly drive cost, not ingredients.

Pharmacy pouch packagers are the scale tool: BD Parata's ATP 2 runs 64 to 480 canisters ([BD](https://www.bd.com/en-ca/products-and-solutions/products/product-page.901-0238)), Parata markets vision verification directly to nutraceutical brands ([Parata](https://parata.com/who-we-help/solutions-for-the-nutraceutical-market/)), and used units have sold for $2,500 at a government auction and up to $49,900 on eBay ([GSA](https://ppms.gov/auctions/preview/341998); [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/shop/parata-machine?_nkw=parata+machine)). The only throughput figure found, 10 to 20 pouches a minute for a small unit (marketplace listing, unverified) ([Alibaba](https://m.alibaba.com/product/1600484075402/JVM-TCG-Parata-High-Quality-Automatic.html)), implies about two machines on one shift for 10,000 customers (est.) and makes owned equipment hard to justify below 2,000 to 3,000 subscribers.

### Vilo owns quality and adverse-event duties even when it outsources

Outsourcing does not outsource responsibility. FDA warning letters call the own-label distributor "ultimately responsible" for Part 111 compliance ([SupplySide SJ](https://www.supplysidesj.com/manufacturing/own-label-distributors-and-contract-manufacturers-a-complex-relationship-requiring-mutual-support)), so Vilo needs its own quality unit, contractor audits and a quality agreement, and review of master manufacturing records. Serious adverse events must reach FDA within **15 business days** on MedWatch 3500A, with records kept 6 years and a domestic contact on the label ([21 U.S.C. 379aa-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/379aa-1)). Prop 65's lead safe harbor is **0.5 µg a day**, computed at the labeled daily serving ([Eurofins](https://www.eurofins.com/en-us/food-feed-testing/resource-library/proposition-65-in-food-dietary-supplements-key-risks-limits-and-compliance/); [CA AG](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/prop65/settlements/2025-03514S7722.pdf)); because a personalized pack sums lead from every unit, the engine should store lead per unit from each certificate of analysis and cap the daily total, or show a warning before purchase. Certifying a variable pack is impractical, so certify the 20 to 40 component SKUs and require NSF/ANSI 455-2 at the manufacturer and packer, which takes 4 to 18 months ([NSF](https://d2evkimvhatqav.cloudfront.net/documents/hs_dce_1911_gmp_455_faq_s.pdf)); certification cost estimates conflict by 2x or more, such as $40,000 to $80,000 in year one for a 4-SKU NSF Certified for Sport program ([V5 Ultimate](https://v5ultimate.com/guides/supplement-third-party-testing-compared)), so get quotes. Budget $30,000 to $80,000 for one-time quality and regulatory setup at MVP (est.).

### Labels, shipping and summer heat

Every shipment is a labeled supplement whose Supplement Facts must match that customer's exact amounts. The workable design prints a variable-data panel, ingredient list, DSHEA disclaimer, distributor and adverse-event contact, lot and expiry on the outer box, and treats daily pouches as inner units under FDA's multi-unit provision, which lets units not sold separately omit the panel ([FDA Labeling Guide](https://www.fda.gov/food/dietary-supplements-guidance-documents-regulatory-information/dietary-supplement-labeling-guide-chapter-iv-nutrition-labeling)); counsel must confirm this applies to sachets. Each pouch should print her name, date and AM or PM, and the record should link every component lot, the packager and canister, so an adverse event or recall can be traced. Pack expiry is the earliest component expiry, and pouch film needs a stability rationale for moisture-sensitive items.

A 30-day box weighs about 7 to 12 ounces (est.) and ships USPS Ground Advantage commercial for **$7.33 to $8.95** across zones on the Oct 4, 2026 list ([USPS Notice 123](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/notice123.htm); [DimMath](https://dimmath.com/blog/usps-ground-advantage-rates-zone-chart-weight-breaks/)), though earlier 2026 lists conflict (unverified) ([Flipper Tools](https://flippertools.com/tools/usps-ground-advantage-calculator)). All parcels from 4 to 15.999 ounces price the same, so packaging weight barely matters and a central-US ship point that shortens zones does. Third-party logistics pick and pack runs about $2 to $5 an order plus $0.75 to $3 for kitting, with monthly minimums averaging $752 ([Fulfill.com](https://www.fulfill.com/3pl-pricing); [Rush Order](https://rushorder.com/blog/how-much-does-a-3pl-cost)). Softgels and probiotics suffer most in summer ground shipping above about 85°F ([BodyBio](https://bodybio.com/pages/shipping-in-summer-months-faq)); Pure Encapsulations never ships probiotics on Thursday or Friday and prefers foam bricks to ice packs ([Pure Encapsulations](https://blog.pureencapsulationspro.com/?p=404)). The cheaper answer is a shelf-stable or spore-based probiotic in moisture-protective packaging, or none in the base product. Personalized packs can't be resold, so returns are refunded or reshipped and destroyed with a log entry.

### Inventory discipline and the Care/of lesson

At scale, inventory is a 20 to 40 SKU formulary held as finished units. Contract-manufacturer minimums of 150,000 to 350,000 bulk capsules or 1,000 to 5,000 bottles per custom SKU ([Superior](https://www.superiorsupplementmfg.com/custom-capsule-manufacturing/); [Sun Nutraceuticals](https://sunnutra.com/low-moq-supplement-manufacturer)) put about **$135,000 to $225,000** into a 30-SKU formulary (est.), and long-tail SKUs can expire before they sell. Start with 15 to 25 SKUs, buy long-tail items as stock white-label products, personalize with multiples of one strength (one to three units of D3 1,000 IU rather than three strengths), forecast from the engine's deterministic output distribution, and reorder on expiry as well as quantity.

Care/of is the cautionary tale. Bayer bought 70% for about $225 million in 2020 ([Bayer](https://www.bayer.com/media/en-us/bayer-acquires-majority-stake-in-careof/)), then shut it in June 2024, cancelling all subscriptions and laying off 143 people ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/careof-is-shutting-down/719106)), after a drift into Target, Amazon and Sam's Club ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/06/07/Care-of-to-close-operations/)). No operational post-mortem exists, but both large pack brands moved toward standardized retail SKUs and B2B channels: Persona added a white-label service in 2024, a practitioner channel, and in January 2026 a new non-personalized brand for younger women ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/01/16/persona-introduces-supplement-brand-for-genz-and-millennial-women)). That pattern says per-customer pack economics are hard at consumer prices on their own, which is why a high-fixed-cost pack operation needs either scale or a partner who shares the fixed costs.

## Claims and ad pixels, not ingredients, create the legal exposure

### The supplement is ordinary; disease claims make it a drug

Vilo's formula is an ordinary dietary supplement: no premarket approval, but structure/function claims must be truthful and substantiated, carry the DSHEA disclaimer, and be notified to FDA within 30 days of first use ([21 U.S.C. 343](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/343); [21 CFR 101.93](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-101/subpart-F/section-101.93)). FDA's December 2025 letter relaxed only where the disclaimer must appear on the label, not whether it appears ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/information-industry-dietary-supplements/letter-dietary-supplement-industry-dshea-disclaimer)). The trap is disease language, and women's health is full of it. FDA treated an inositol product as an unapproved drug for claiming to improve fertility in women with PCOS ([FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/fdc-nutrition-inc-612264-02182021)); a joint FDA/FTC letter targeted fertility-supplement claims about PCOS, luteal phase defect and IVF ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/warning-letters/fda-conceivemedia.pdf)); and TINA.org sent letters to menopause-supplement brands over hot-flash claims in 2024 ([TINA.org](https://truthinadvertising.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/10_7_24-Notification-Letters-to-Menopause-Supplement-Companies_Combined.pdf)).

The app's personalized messages count as labeling or advertising. "Your ferritin result led us to add iron to support healthy energy" is the ceiling; "treats iron-deficiency anemia," "for PCOS" or "improves fertility" are off-limits everywhere, including testimonials, influencer posts and SEO pages. **Keep a finite, counsel-approved claim library, file one 30-day notification for it, and never let a generative model write new claims.** Use only ingredients with clear pre-1994 or NDI-notified status and avoid peptides, NMN-type contested ingredients and hormone precursors; FDA's NMN reversal ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/10/fda-declares-nicotinamide-mononucleotide-is)) and its March 2026 meeting on the scope of dietary ingredients ([Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/fda-modernize-dietary-supplement-regulation)) show the ground is moving, and a deregulatory FDA does nothing to reduce FTC, state attorney general or class-action risk. Mandatory product listing remains a bill, not law ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/01/15/sen-durbin-introduces-dietary-supplement-listing-act-of-2026/)).

### FTC substantiation reaches the word "personalized"

The FTC is the main federal enforcer, and its 2022 Health Products Compliance Guidance requires competent and reliable scientific evidence before a health claim runs ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Health-Products-Compliance-Guidance.pdf)). The closest precedent is **GeneLink/foru**, which sold "genetically customized" supplements for $100 or more a month to about 30,000 consumers; the 2014 orders barred unsupported disease claims and imposed a security program with biennial audits for 20 years ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2014/01/companies-pitching-genetically-customized-nutritional-supplements)). That case tested the personalization premise itself, so "formulated for your biology to fix your fatigue" or "better than a generic multi" needs outcome evidence Vilo doesn't have. Describe the process instead ("your formula is selected from your answers and lab values using published dietary reference intakes"); say "doctor-designed" only if physicians actually designed it; never say "clinically proven" without trials on the formula. The FTC sent its first Consumer Review Rule warning letters to 10 companies in December 2025, with supplements flagged as exposed ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-signals-heightened-enforcement-of-new)), so don't incentivize positive reviews or suppress negative ones. On subscriptions, the federal click-to-cancel rule was vacated in July 2025 and the FTC restarted rulemaking in March 2026, but ROSCA and Section 5 still apply ([Gibson Dunn](https://gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/)); build to California's AB 2863, in force since July 1, 2025, which requires express consent to renewal terms, online cancellation in the same medium as sign-up, and annual reminders ([Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect)).

### The lab-interpretation engine is where device risk lives

FDA's January 6, 2026 General Wellness and Clinical Decision Support guidances loosened the rules for wearables and clinician-facing software ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/01/fda-cuts-red-tape-on-clinical-decision-support-software)), but a wellness product still may not name a condition, call a value abnormal, or give clinical thresholds or treatment advice, though it may suggest seeing a clinician ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/fda-guidance-eases-wearables-oversight-but-experts-have-questions-about-wh/810581/); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-20-fda-opens-aperture-for-wearables-in-latest-general-wellness-guidance)). The statutory CDS exclusion covers only software that supports a health care professional who can independently review its basis ([21 U.S.C. 360j](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/360j)). That places Vilo's most valuable function, lab-guided iron dosing, on the riskiest practical rung.

| Rung | Function | Device risk |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Questionnaire-based nutrient recommendations against dietary reference intakes | Low: general wellness |
| 2 | Wearable sleep, HRV and cycle tips with no disease naming | Low under the 2026 guidance |
| 3 | Showing lab values with reference ranges and nutrition education | Moderate |
| 4 | **Patient-facing algorithm that flags a "deficiency" and auto-sets a corrective dose** | **High:** interprets lab results to identify a condition and recommend treatment, outside the clinician-facing exclusion |
| 5 | Naming or screening conditions (anemia, PCOS, hypothyroidism, perimenopause staging, fertile windows for contraception) | Device function; Natural Cycles is FDA-cleared for exactly this kind of use |

The resolution is the physician network that already orders the labs. A clinician reviews results and approves lab-driven changes through a clinician-facing tool built to meet the CDS criteria, while the consumer sees nutrient-intake language and "consider discussing this with your clinician" for out-of-range values (counsel must confirm against the full 2026 texts, which were not fetched). The clinician layer is where compliance, corporate-practice rules, safety and trust converge: **45% of US adults would be comfortable with AI supplement recommendations if a doctor, pharmacist or dietitian reviewed them** ([CRN 2026](https://crnusa.org/node/7753)), so the review is a selling point, not only a cost.

### Privacy law treats cycle data as class-action fuel

As a cash-pay consumer app, Vilo is very likely not a HIPAA covered entity, though it can become a business associate for the data it handles on behalf of the ordering medical group ([HHS OCR](https://hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr-health-app-developer-scenarios-2-2016.pdf)). Being outside HIPAA puts it under a stricter, litigation-heavy patchwork. The FTC's amended Health Breach Notification Rule treats unauthorized sharing with ad platforms as a breach ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/30/2024-10855/health-breach-notification-rule)), and GoodRx paid $1.5 million in the first case ([Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/08/ftc-updated-health-breach-notification-rule)). In *Frasco v. Flo Health*, a jury found **Meta liable in August 2025** under California's wiretap law for collecting Flo users' reproductive data through its SDK, a statute carrying $5,000 in statutory damages per violation, and Google, Flo and Flurry settled for **$59.5 million** combined ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/meta-found-liable-abusing-consumer-reproductive-health-data-run-targeted-ads); [Data Privacy + Security Insider](https://www.dataprivacyandsecurityinsider.com/2025/08/federal-jury-finds-against-meta-for-collecting-data-from-flo-health/)). Washington's My Health My Data Act gives consumers a private right of action and already has its first class action ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20250220-first-lawsuit-filed-under-washingtons-my-health-my-data-act)); Maryland's MODPA bans selling consumer health data even with consent and limits collection to what is strictly necessary from April 1, 2026 ([Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/privacy-and-data-security/now-in-effect-maryland-law-raises-bar-on-sensitive-data-data-minimization-and-children-s-privacy)); Nevada and Connecticut add consumer-health-data rules ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/connecticut-and-nevada-legislatures-pass-health-data-laws)); and California's AB 254 brings reproductive-health apps under its medical privacy law ([Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2024/01/31/state-privacy-trends-in-reproductive-health)). New York's revised Health Information Privacy Act passed both houses in June 2026 and awaited the governor in the latest sources ([Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/07/23/new-york-renews-push-to-enact-consumer-health-information-privacy-act-nyhipa)). The 2024 HIPAA reproductive-health rule was vacated nationwide in June 2025 ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/federal-court-vacates-2024-hipaa-reproductive-health-privacy-rule/)), so cycle data in a consumer app has no HIPAA shield against subpoenas.

The rule that matters most is simple: **no third-party ad pixels or SDKs on any logged-in, quiz, results or checkout screen, and no custom audiences built from health inputs.** Use server-side analytics with an event allowlist, separate opt-in consents for collection and for each sharing purpose, a Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy, no sale of health data anywhere, and treat inferences such as "likely perimenopausal" as sensitive data. Keep cycle logs on the device or end-to-end encrypted with short retention, send the server derived flags rather than raw logs where possible, avoid precise geolocation, and publish a warrant-required law-enforcement policy with a transparency report. Build to New York's bill now rather than re-architect later. The data-minimization rule and the science agree: since most signals don't change the formula, Vilo can collect less and lose nothing.

### Practice-of-medicine, dietitian, Prop 65, age and HSA rules

Individualized nutrition advice is a state patchwork. California expressly allows general nutrition advice that includes dietary supplements ([Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://www.eatrightpro.org/advocacy/licensure/licensure-map-and-statutes-by-state/california)), while an advocacy chart lists Alabama, Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota and Tennessee as states where non-dietitian specialists cannot give individualized counseling ([CNS chart](https://www.theana.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/CNS-State-By-State-Practie-Rights_Updated-Nov-2024-1.pdf)). Offer 1:1 coaching only through licensed dietitians, frame engine output as product selection, and geo-limit coaching in those states if counsel advises. Age-gate at 18: New York has banned selling weight-loss and muscle-building supplements to minors since April 2024, with creatine and green tea extract cited as possible triggers ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/04/25/new-york-outlaws-sales-of-diet-pills-muscle-mass-builders-children/)). Product liability for small supplement brands runs about $2,800 to $4,500 a year per one broker ([Insurance Canopy](https://www.insurancecanopy.com/vitamins-and-dietary-supplements-insurance)); add recall cover, cyber cover that explicitly includes privacy-litigation defense (check wiretap and MHMDA exclusions), tech errors and omissions for engine mistakes, and malpractice in the professional entity. On HSA/FSA, a letter of medical necessity requires a disease purpose that collides with structure/function positioning, and Truemed-style programs, though mainstream among wellness brands ([NutraIngredients-USA](https://nutraingredients-usa.com/Article/2024/10/22/Truemed-unlocks-HSA-FSA-coverage-for-dietary-supplements)), carry audit risk for customers; say at most "may be eligible with a letter of medical necessity; check your plan."

### EU and UK: authorized claims, national limits, explicit consent

Europe is a separate product, not a translation. Only authorized health claims are allowed, such as "iron contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue," with no US-style structure/function freedom ([Leatherhead](https://www.leatherheadfood.com/news-detail/blog-regulations-impact-personalised-nutrition/)). EU-wide maximum vitamin and mineral levels were never adopted, so national limits apply ([Directive 2002/46/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ%3AL%3A2002%3A183%3A0051%3A0057%3AEN%3APDF)), and Germany, Italy and France require product notification, which raises the open question of whether each personalized combination needs its own filing; firms usually notify a defined set of modules ([BVL](https://verwaltung.bund.de/leistungsverzeichnis/en/leistung/99118031261000); [NutraIngredients 2026](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/05/19/european-expansion-through-replication-the-costly-regulatory-pitfall/)). The engine needs a per-market rules table using the stricter limits, notably EFSA's 12 mg B6 UL and 40 mg iron safe level. Health data is GDPR Article 9 special-category data, so explicit consent is generally the only lawful basis, and the CJEU has treated data from e-commerce sales of health products as health data ([Vitafoods Insights](https://www.vitafoodsinsights.com/personalised-nutrition/what-does-gdpr-mean-for-personalised-nutrition-); [Portolano Cavallo](https://portolano.it/en/blog/life-sciences/do-users-data-processed-ecommerce-operators-qualify-health-data-ecj-says-yes-)); the engine could also be a medical device under EU MDR Rule 11 (unverified). The UK has no statutory maximum levels and uses the 2003 expert-group guidance instead ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/food-supplements-guidance-and-faqs/guidance-notes-on-legislation-implementing-directive-200246ec-on-food-supplements)), keeps an authorized-claims regime and UK GDPR, and applies MHRA borderline-medicine scrutiny under Guidance Note 8, updated August 2026 ([MHRA](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a747082bde92a23046b246e/GN8_FINAL_20260806.pdf)); Great Britain reportedly requires no supplement notification (unverified). If Vilo goes abroad, the UK is the easier first stop.

## The model clears 3x only with CAC under $150 and churn under 7%

### Acquisition is expensive and retention is fragile

Health and wellness carries the highest median Meta CPM in Triple Whale's data, **$22.44 against $15.72** across all industries, with a median cost per acquisition of $42.30 ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)); Varos put supplement cost per purchase at $45.62 ([Foundry CRO](https://foundrycro.com/blog/dtc-supplements-marketing-benchmarks-2026/)). Those are first purchases, not retained subscribers; for a quiz-plus-test funnel the research team estimates **$80 to $250 per paying subscriber** (est.). Recharge data show **26.5% of health and wellness subscribers drop at first renewal**, against 7.5% for mature subscribers ([Recharge](https://getrecharge.com/?p=27190)), and the category had the highest churn of any subscription category in 2022, near 10% a month ([Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1419950/subscription-commerce-churn-rate-category)). Vendor benchmarks put supplement steady-state churn at 5 to 8% a month and month-6 retention at 45 to 62%, and claim annual billing keeps about 96% of 12-month revenue against about 70% for monthly (unverified vendor claims) ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/supplement-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark)).

### The research team's scenario model

The 36-month cohort model below excludes fixed costs and assumes goods-plus-fulfillment of $15 to $24 a month, which implies scale manufacturing. All outputs are estimates.

| Input or output | Pessimistic | Base | Optimistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supplement price per month | $55 | $69 | $89 |
| Test price to customer / cost to Vilo | $49 / $110 | $99 / $85 | $129 / $70 |
| CAC per paying subscriber | $250 | $150 | $120 |
| Churn: month 1 / months 2 and 3 / steady | 28% / 15% / 10% | 20% / 12% / 7% | 15% / 8% / 4.5% |
| Goods and fulfillment per month | $24 | $18 | $15 |
| Active at month 12 | 22% | 35% | 50% |
| 36-month contribution LTV | $66 | $443 | $1,083 |
| **LTV:CAC** | **0.26x** | **2.96x** | **9.0x** |
| **Payback** | **Never** | **5 months** | **2 months** |

The sensitivities are blunt. A free test cuts the base case from 3.0x to 2.3x and stretches payback to 9 months; a $149 test lifts it to 3.3x. At $150 CAC, $49 a month yields 1.6x and $89 yields 4.3x. Churn and CAC dominate everything: 4.5% steady churn at $100 CAC gives 5.6x, while 10% churn at $250 CAC gives 1.4x. A quiz-only tier at $59 with $110 CAC reaches 2.7x but keeps only 26% of subscribers at month 12; an all-in $99 membership including two tests reaches 3.6x at $200 CAC. The research also estimates that at about $45 a month of contribution, Vilo needs **20,000 to 25,000 active subscribers** to cover a lean $12 million a year of fixed costs (est.).

### Outsourced MVP packs break the base case

Here two research tracks collide, and the founder should see it plainly. The economics model assumed $18 a month for goods and fulfillment; the manufacturing track estimates **$35 to $44 a month** landed for outsourced per-customer packs at MVP volume. Extrapolating the model's own COGS sensitivity (each extra $6 of monthly COGS cost 0.4 to 0.5x of LTV:CAC), the base case falls to roughly **1.8x at $35 COGS and 1.4x at $40** (my extrapolation, est.). Raising the price to $89 at $40 COGS restores roughly 2.8x, assuming churn doesn't rise with price, which is optimistic; the $59 quiz-only tier at outsourced cost falls to about 1x. The implication: **the MVP is a learning phase, not a profit engine.** Price the lab tier near $89 a month or sell annual prepay, cap paid acquisition until cohort data arrive, and reach hybrid manufacturing (COGS toward $18 to $25, est.) before scaling spend.

### Levers ranked by impact

In order of impact, the levers are steady-state churn and CAC (together they swing the base case from 1.4x to 5.6x), then price (each $10 a month moves roughly 0.6 to 0.7x), then annual prepay, which pays back on a cash basis inside month one, then test pricing (free versus $99 is worth 0.7x), and COGS last once at scale. The retest loop is the hypothesis to test first: if a month-3 to month-4 retest that visibly re-formulates the pack moved steady churn from 7% to 4.5%, LTV:CAC would rise from 3.0x to 3.7x at $150 CAC, worth up to about $100 per subscriber, so a retest costing $85 or less roughly pays for itself (est.; no public data proves the effect). HSA/FSA eligibility of the itemized lab line acts as a 20 to 35% discount on the test for eligible buyers at their marginal tax rate (est.).

Six decisions carry most of the outcome, ranked with the best option first.

**First, lead with labs but never make labs a prerequisite.** The decisive tradeoff is friction (an estimated 25 to 50% of buyers won't complete a draw) against differentiation: a quiz-only pack competes head-on with $43 to $63 retail women's multis and replays Care/of, while a lab-adjusted second box lands exactly when 26.5% of health subscribers normally quit.

**Second, outsource pack assembly until 2,000 to 5,000 subscribers.** You pay several times the in-house cost per pack to avoid capex, inventory and a packing facility while the formulary is still changing.

**Third, charge for the test near cost.** Free testing lifts conversion but costs 0.7x of LTV:CAC in the base case; a $99 to $149 test also screens for intent.

**Fourth, put a clinician in the loop for every lab-driven change.** It costs a per-review fee (benchmark $25, unverified) and corporate-practice structuring, and it buys device-risk mitigation, critical-value coverage, and a trust signal that 45% of adults respond to.

**Fifth, ship native HealthKit and Health Connect only at MVP.** You give up direct Garmin, WHOOP and Oura metrics, which don't change doses anyway, and save $300 to $450 a month in aggregator fees plus about $1 per connected user.

**Sixth, push annual prepay.** Monthly headline revenue is lower, but cash arrives up front and vendor data claim far better 12-month revenue retention. At MVP pack costs, an $899 prepaid year roughly covers a year of packs, two tests and a $150 CAC with little left over (est.), so its value at MVP is cash and retention, not margin.

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the opportunity. Vilo's defensible product is not an AI that reads a woman's body in real time; it is a safety-and-iron engine wrapped in a life-stage formula, made credible by a clinician and made sticky by retests that visibly change the box. That is a narrower story than the category's marketing tells, but it is the one that survives an FTC investigator, an FDA reviewer and a plaintiff's lawyer, and it is also cheaper to collect and to manufacture, because the few inputs that carry the science map directly onto a base-plus-add-ons pack. The same minimalism that protects users' privacy protects the margin.

Two implications deserve the founder's attention. First, the most valuable asset Vilo can build is evidence: a pragmatic trial showing that more women reach ferritin of 30 ng/mL or above on a lab-guided formula than on a standard multi would be the first of its kind in the category, would unlock claims no competitor can make, and would make Vilo the natural formulation layer for platforms (Oura, Superpower, Function, Hims & Hers) that already own testing and members but lack women's formulation depth. Second, sequence beats any single choice: hybrid manufacturing has to arrive before paid acquisition scales, or Vilo repeats the Care/of pattern of buying customers it cannot serve at a margin.
