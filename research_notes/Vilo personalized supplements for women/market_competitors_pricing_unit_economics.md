## 1. Competitor landscape: pricing, business model, funding, status; who combines testing with personalized supplements

### Takeaway
The pure-play "quiz → personalized daily pack" model has largely failed or been absorbed:
- Care/of was shut down by Bayer in 2024.
- Baze stopped blood testing in 2022.
- Persona sits quietly inside Nestlé.

Two groups now hold the momentum:
- **Lab-membership platforms that sell supplements on the side:** Function Health ($365/yr), Superpower ($199 to $349/yr plus a supplement marketplace), Hims & Hers Labs ($199 or $499/yr), and Oura Health Panels ($99).
- **Women-specific non-personalized brands that went into retail:** Ritual (5,000+ doors), Perelel, and Needed.

Few US players truly combine their own blood test with a formulated-for-you supplement: Rootine, Bioniq PRO, historically Baze, and Viome (gut/RNA tests, not blood). None is women-specific at scale. That gap is Vilo's opening, and it is also a warning sign.

### Inferences
- **Price anchors a US woman sees in 2026:**
  - Non-personalized women's multis/prenatals: ~$43 to $63/mo (Needed, Ritual, Perelel).
  - Quiz-personalized packs: ~$40 to $100/mo (Persona, Elix, Bioniq GO $75).
  - Blood-informed personalized supplements: ~$60 to $94/mo plus a $125 to $338 test (Rootine).
  - Annual lab memberships: $199 to $499/yr (Superpower, Hims Labs, Function).
  - A $99 single panel from Oura.
- A Vilo supplement at ~$59 to $79/mo sits mid-market. Including testing makes it look expensive next to $199 to $365 lab memberships unless the test is clearly bundled.
- **Biggest competitive threat: horizontal platforms adding supplements.** Hims & Hers, Function, Superpower and Oura already own testing plus a large member base, and Hers is already women-focused. Superpower's supplement marketplace shows the path. If Hims/Hers or Oura launched lab-driven women's supplements, Vilo's differentiation would compress quickly.
- **Women-specific, non-personalized brands win on retail distribution, not personalization.** Ritual (5,000+ doors, $250M+ revenue) and AG1 ($600M) both moved from DTC-only to mass retail in 2025 to 2026. This suggests DTC-only subscription economics alone were not sufficient even for category leaders.

## 2. Why personalized supplement startups failed or were absorbed (Care/of, Baze, others)

### Takeaway
No public post-mortem gives Care/of's CAC, churn or margins. The documented causes are:
- declining revenue;
- a corporate parent (Bayer) in restructuring mode under Roundup litigation pressure;
- a shift into retail that diluted the subscription and personalization premise.

The category pattern is consistent across Care/of, Baze and Nestlé's VMS retreat. Acquisition costs are high (health and wellness has the highest Meta CPMs of any category). Churn is front-loaded: roughly 26.5% of health and wellness subscribers drop at first renewal. And consumers can buy look-alike multivitamins in retail at much lower prices. Testing adds cost and logistics friction; Baze dropped its blood test.

### Cited Findings
- **Bayer's stated reason:** ending investment in Care/of "will allow Bayer to better invest in future innovations." The closure coincided with a Bayer restructuring announced January 2024 to scale back investment and remove management layers ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/06/07/Care-of-to-close-operations/)).
- **Revenue was falling:** an unnamed source close to Care/of acknowledged revenue was down but did not say by how much. Bayer's consumer health division itself was profitable. Bayer's wider difficulties were tied to Roundup litigation ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/06/07/Care-of-to-close-operations/); [Ingredients Network](https://ingredientsnetwork.com/bayer-shutters-its-personalised-supplement-news124945.html)).
- **Cause is contested:** coverage notes it is "unresolved" whether the closure was restructuring, low revenue, or both ([NutraIngredients-USA, "Care/of's rise and fall"](https://www.nutraingredients-usa.com/Article/2024/06/28/The-rise-and-fall-of-Care-of-A-financial-perspective), via search summary only; full article not readable).
- **Closure notice:** coverage of the New York WARN notice says it cited a "funding loss"; 143 employees; subscriptions cancelled June 17, 2024 ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/careof-is-shutting-down/719106)).
- **Retail drift:** Care/of went into Target (2021), then Amazon and Sam's Club, moving away from the quiz-subscription core ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/06/07/Care-of-to-close-operations/); [HulkApps analysis](https://www.hulkapps.com/blogs/ecommerce-hub/the-shuttering-of-care-of-an-in-depth-analysis-of-the-vitamin-subscription-companys-closure)). This is a secondary blog, not a primary source.
- **Care/of's CEO in 2020:** acknowledged online acquisition prices were "extremely high" but argued personalization made the model defensible (search summary attributing an interview; [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1514002)). Exact wording was not verified.
- **Baze:** blood-test-plus-vitamin startup backed by Nature's Way ($6M, 2019) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/08/30/at-home-blood-testing-startup-baze-rakes-in-6-million-from-natures-way/amp/)). - **Nestlé's VMS retreat:**
  - In July 2025 Nestlé launched a strategic review of its "mainstream and value" VMS brands (Nature's Bounty, Puritan's Pride and others), citing weaker performance and lost private-label business ([FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/07/25/nestle-weighs-selling-supplement-assets/); [BNN Bloomberg/Reuters](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2025/07/24/nestle-announces-vitamins-business-review-after-sluggish-sales-growth/)).
  - It later agreed to sell the "Holistic Health portfolio" to Yellow Wood Partners for ~SFr800M (~$1B), against roughly CHF1B / $1.2B in annual sales, keeping premium brands Solgar and Pure Encapsulations ([Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/newsletters/nestle-to-sell-mainstream-supplements-business-for-1bn)). Those assets came from the $5.75B Bountiful deal in 2021.
- **Category churn is structural:** in Recharge data, health and wellness had the highest churn of any subscription category in 2022, near 10%/month ([Statista citing Recharge](https://statista.com/statistics/1419950/subscription-commerce-churn-rate-category)). H&W first-renewal churn (cycles 2 to 3) was 26.5%, against 7.5% for mature subscribers ([Recharge blog](https://getrecharge.com/?p=27190), via search summary).
- **Ad costs:** health and wellness has the highest median Meta CPM in Triple Whale's dataset: $22.44 (Nov 2025 to Oct 2026, +14% YoY) against $15.72 across all industries ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)).

### Inferences
- **The common failure mechanism is CAC × churn.** Expensive paid social (highest-CPM category), plus a 20 to 27% drop at first renewal, plus a product consumers see as a commodity multivitamin, produces sub-1x to ~2x LTV:CAC unless retention is unusually good. The pessimistic case in Q7 reproduces this.
- **Corporate acquirers tolerate small, sub-scale DTC units only briefly.** Bayer (Care/of), Nestlé (Persona, sitting quietly) and Pharmavite (Nurish, silent) suggest strategics value the data and the brand, but cut once growth stalls. Vilo should not plan around a strategic exit as a safety net.
- **Personalization alone is weak perceived value.** When retailers stock comparable SKUs (Care/of's own retail push is the tell), the personalization premium erodes. Real lab data plus visible re-formulation from retest results is the clearest way to differentiate. Baze shows the test logistics themselves can break the model if priced and staffed poorly.

### Gaps
- No primary post-mortem with Care/of's CAC, churn, gross margin, revenue trajectory or subscriber counts was found. The NutraIngredients "financial perspective" piece could not be fetched.
- The reasons Baze stopped testing (cost, regulation, demand) are not documented in press.
- No public data was found on other 2024 to 2026 personalized-vitamin shutdowns; the search budget ran out before this could be checked.

## 4. DTC subscription benchmarks: CAC, churn, retention, margins, payback, LTV:CAC; effect of blood testing

### Takeaway
| Metric | Benchmark range |
|---|---|
| Meta cost per purchase, health and wellness / supplements | ~$40 to $46 (first purchase, not subscriber) |
| Fully loaded CAC per subscriber, personalized/quiz/test funnel | Plausibly $80 to $250 (estimate) |
| Steady-state monthly churn, H&W subscriptions | 5 to 10% (top quartile <3%) |
| Early churn | 20 to 27% at first renewal |
| Month-6 retention | ~45 to 62% |
| Month-12 retention (implied) | ~25 to 40% |
| Target LTV:CAC | ≥3:1 |

There is **no public data quantifying how a blood test changes conversion or retention.** Companies' heavy investment in retest loops (Function and Hims Advanced at 2 draws/yr; InsideTracker multi-test bundles) is circumstantial evidence only.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta ad costs (Triple Whale):** health and wellness median CPM $22.44 (Nov 2025 to Oct 2026, +14.06%), the highest of any industry; all-industry median $15.72; **H&W median CPA $42.30 (+10.73%)** ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)). Rolling 90-day H&W CPM median $22.05 as of Sept 2026 ([Triple Whale Labs](https://benchmark.triplewhalelabs.com/benchmarks/health_and_wellness/cpm/)).
- **Varos, April 2025 (vitamins and supplements):** Meta CPM $17.78, CTR 1.01%, **median cost per purchase $45.62** ([Foundry CRO citing Varos](https://foundrycro.com/blog/dtc-supplements-marketing-benchmarks-2026/)).
- **Blended DTC supplement CAC:** "commonly cited around $89" ([Commerce Catalyst](https://commercecatalyst.ai/benchmarks/supplements/)). Vendor blog; uncorroborated.
- **Recharge churn data:**
  - H&W subscription churn was ~8 to 10%/month through 2022 (10.1% in January 2022), the highest category ([Statista citing Recharge](https://statista.com/statistics/1419950/subscription-commerce-churn-rate-category); [Recharge industry report](https://getrecharge.com/resources/industry-report/)).
  - **H&W first-renewal churn (cycles 2 to 3): 26.5% vs 7.5% for mature (cycle 6+) subscribers** ([Recharge blog](https://getrecharge.com/?p=27190)).
  - H&W average order value: $59 (2022) to $63 (2023) ([Recharge](https://getrecharge.com/?p=23884)).
- **Vendor benchmark blogs** (treat as directional):
  - Supplement subscription monthly churn 5 to 8% (citing Platform DTC); top quartile <3%; month-1 churn 20 to 25%; ~45 to 62% retained at month 6 ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/supplement-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark); [Platform DTC](https://platformdtc.com/answers/subscription-churn-rate-benchmarks)).
  - Annual billing ≈96% 12-month net revenue retention vs ≈70% for monthly billing ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/supplement-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark)).
  - LTV:CAC of 3:1 as the floor and 3.5 to 6:1 as healthy; above ~16% monthly churn a 6-month CAC payback fails ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/subscription-churn)).
- **Retest loops are productized:**
  - Function and Superpower memberships include draws; Superpower is annual with add-on panels at extra cost ([Superpower](https://superpower.com/baseline-membership); [Crown Counseling](https://crowncounseling.com/reviews/superpower-health-review/)).
  - Hims Labs Advanced includes 2 draws per year ([Hims newsroom](https://news.hims.com/newsroom/introducing-labs-by-hims-hers)).
  - InsideTracker sells 2-test ($761) and 4-test ($1,305) bundles ([Superpower comparison](https://superpower.com/biomarker-testing-companies/superpower-vs-insidetracker)).
  - Baze charged $99 per retest ([Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/p/the-baze-starter-kit-is-a-new-vitamin-personalization-service-the-tells-you-exactly-what-youre-deficient-in-12273497)).
- **Hims & Hers scale benchmark:** ~2.8M subscribers; Q3 2026 revenue $890M ([Hims IR](https://investors.hims.com/news/news-details/2026/Hims--Hers-Health-Inc--Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)). CAC was not captured.

### Inferences
- **Meta CPA of ~$40 to $46 is the cost of a first purchase, not a retained subscriber.** For a high-consideration quiz-plus-test funnel, cost per paying subscriber will likely be 2 to 5x that, about $90 to $250 (estimate). The figure gets worse if the test is sold separately and only a fraction of test buyers subscribe. For example, $90 per test buyer at 60% test-to-subscription conversion equals $150 per subscriber.
- **Gross margins (estimate):** personalized-pack DTC brands typically run ~65 to 80% product gross margin before fulfillment, and ~55 to 75% after pick, pack and ship (see Q7). No public company-specific margins were found for personalized packs.
- **Hypotheses for how a blood test could lift retention** (no public data supports these):
  - an objective "before/after" retest at 3 to 6 months;
  - sunk-cost and identity effects;
  - re-formulation events that reset the subscription.
  - Testing also raises CAC (price shock, friction) and adds variable cost. Model it as a lever, not an assumption (see the Q7 variants).
- **Annual prepay is the strongest documented retention lever** (vendor claim of ~96% vs ~70% NRR). The Function, Superpower and Hims Labs annual memberships align with this.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025 to 2026 Recharge category churn figures were found; the newest are 2022 to 2023. The Recharge Subscription Trend Report 2026 could not be read.
- No TikTok or influencer CAC benchmarks specific to supplements were found.
- No public data on test-to-subscription conversion, or on retention lift from retesting, was found for Rootine, InsideTracker, Baze or others.
- No Common Thread Collective benchmark was retrieved.

## 5. Pricing architecture options and what comparables charge for each component

### Takeaway
The market has converged on these component prices:
- **Annual lab membership:** $199 to $499/yr (Superpower $199 to $349, Function $365, Hims Labs $199/$499, InsideTracker ~$489 to $699).
- **Single panel:** ~$99 (Oura).
- **Specialty test:** $129 to $159 (Evvy).
- **Personalized supplement:** $48 to $94/mo.
- **Women's non-personalized brands:** $43 to $63/mo.

Price elasticity evidence is thin, but the direction is clear: lab memberships have been cutting price to broaden reach (Function $499 → $365; Superpower launched at $199). The strongest structural option for Vilo is a tiered architecture: a quiz-based entry tier, plus a lab-driven tier with bundled annual retests and annual prepay.

### Inferences
**Recommended architecture to test** (derived from the comparables and the Q7 model; not sourced):
- **Tier A, "Vilo Core" (quiz + app + wearable data):** ~$49 to $59/mo, priced near Ritual, Perelel and Needed so a lab-free entry exists. Low CAC, higher churn.
- **Tier B, "Vilo Lab" (blood panel + physician review + lab-informed formula):** $129 to $149 upfront test (or $0 with annual prepay), then $69 to $89/mo. Include a free 90- to 120-day retest to create a visible before/after event, and offer annual prepay at ~15 to 20% off.
- **Why not include testing for free:** it damages unit economics. In the Q7 base case, a free test cuts LTV:CAC from 3.0x to 2.3x and stretches payback from 5 to 9 months. Pricing the test at least near cost ($99 to $149) recovers it and screens for higher-intent buyers.
- **Keep the all-in membership price below the stacked price of a lab membership plus a branded supplement.** Superpower at $199 to $349/yr plus a $40 to $60/mo multi comes to roughly $680 to $1,070/yr. A Vilo Lab at ~$79/mo is ~$950/yr, which is defensible only if the formulation and women-specific app (cycle-aware dosing) are clearly better.
- **HSA/FSA eligibility** (Q6) effectively discounts the price 20 to 35% for eligible buyers at their marginal tax rate (estimate). That may be a larger elasticity lever than list price.

### Gaps
- No controlled price-elasticity studies, or published A/B test results, for personalized supplements or lab memberships were found.
- No data was found on what share of lab-membership customers buy supplements through marketplaces (Superpower attach rate unknown).

## 7. Worked unit-economics model (base / optimistic / pessimistic)

### Takeaway
- **Base case** ($69/mo lab-informed formula, $99 test priced near cost, $150 CAC per subscriber, 20% first-renewal churn falling to 7%/mo steady state): ~74% product gross margin, ~$45/mo steady-state contribution, ~$443 36-month contribution LTV. **LTV:CAC ≈ 3.0x, payback ≈ 5 months.** Workable but thin.
- **Pessimistic case** ($55/mo, a subsidized test, $250 CAC, 10%/mo steady churn): the business never pays back (LTV:CAC ≈ 0.3x). This is essentially the Care/of failure mode.
- **Optimistic case** ($89/mo, $129 test with margin, $120 CAC, 4.5% steady churn): ≈9x LTV:CAC and a ~2-month payback.

The two variables that decide the outcome are **CAC per retained subscriber** and **steady-state churn**, followed by price and test pricing. COGS matters less.

### Cited Findings (inputs that are sourced)
- **CAC anchors:** Meta H&W median CPA $42.30 ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)); supplements median cost per purchase $45.62 (Varos, Apr 2025) ([Foundry CRO](https://foundrycro.com/blog/dtc-supplements-marketing-benchmarks-2026/)); blended supplement CAC ~$89 (uncorroborated) ([Commerce Catalyst](https://commercecatalyst.ai/benchmarks/supplements/)).
- **Churn anchors:** H&W first-renewal churn 26.5% and mature churn 7.5% ([Recharge](https://getrecharge.com/?p=27190)); supplement steady-state 5 to 8%, top quartile <3%, month-1 20 to 25%, month-6 retention 45 to 62% ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/supplement-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark)).
- **Price anchors:** see Q1 and Q5. Personalized $48 to $94/mo; test $99 (Oura) to $125 to $338 (Rootine); lab membership $199 to $499/yr.
- **Wearable aggregator cost:**
  - Terra charges 200 credits per connected user per month; credits beyond the included 100k cost $0.005 each, which works out to **~$1.00 per connected user per month**; the Streaming API is a $99/mo add-on ([Terra docs](https://docs.tryterra.co/unified-api/pricing); [apis.io](https://apis.io/plans/terra-api/terra-api-plans-pricing/)).
  - Junction (formerly Vital) moved from $0.50/user/month to a $300/mo flat Launch plan for up to 500 users (~$0.60/user at capacity), with custom Enterprise pricing above that (Aug 2026 change log; not confirmed on Junction's site) ([pricingsaas](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/tryvital/diffs/2026W35)).
- **Lab ordering:**
  - Junction offers pre-negotiated Quest pricing and standard Labcorp pricing, with a **flat collection fee per walk-in order**. Its example reflex-test prices run $17.50 to $21.50. Lab testing sits in the Enterprise plan ([Junction docs](https://docs.junction.com/lab/overview/order-pricing)).
  - Retail price points for a ~50-biomarker panel: Oura $99, Hims Labs Base $199/yr ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/health-panels/); [Hims](https://news.hims.com/newsroom/introducing-labs-by-hims-hers)).
- **Physician review:** Oura uses SteadyMD for critical-result follow-up ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/health-panels/)). Per-order physician fees were **not found**.

### Inferences

#### Model structure
- Cohort expected-value model with a 36-month horizon.
- Churn is applied as: month-1→2 churn (c1), month-2→3 and 3→4 churn (c23), then steady-state monthly churn.
- Payback is the first month in which cumulative expected contribution per acquired subscriber, including the test margin at acquisition, is at least CAC.
- CAC is **per paying supplement subscriber**. In a test-first funnel this equals CAC per test buyer divided by the test-to-subscription conversion rate.
- Excludes fixed costs: R&D, clinical staff salaries, G&A, and formulation/regulatory overhead.
- Payment processing assumed at 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction (Stripe standard US card rate; **not re-verified this session**).

#### Assumptions (E = estimate to be cross-checked by the ops/manufacturing researcher)
| Input | Pessimistic | Base | Optimistic | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supplement price/mo | $55 | $69 | $89 | Comparables $48 to $94 (Q1) |
| First-month discount | 50% | 25% | 15% | Common DTC practice (E) |
| COGS + packaging + pick/pack + shipping /mo | $24 | $18 | $15 | **E.** Ingredients ~$6 to $14, sachets/box $1 to $3, personalized pick/pack $2 to $4, shipping $4 to $7 |
| Payment processing | 2.9% + $0.30 | same | same | Stripe standard rate (assumed) |
| App/data infra per user/mo (wearable aggregator, hosting, AI inference) | $3.00 | $2.00 | $1.50 | Terra ~$1/user + Junction ~$0.60/user + hosting/AI (E) |
| Support/CS per user/mo | $2.00 | $1.50 | $1.00 | E |

| Output | Pessimistic | Base | Optimistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product gross margin (price − COGS/fulfillment) | 56% | 74% | 83% |
| Steady-state contribution per month (after processing, infra, CS) | $24.11 (44%) | $45.20 (66%) | $68.62 (77%) |
| Test margin at acquisition | −$62.72 | +$10.83 | +$54.96 |

| 36-month contribution LTV (incl. tests) | $66 | $443 | $1,083 |
| 12-month contribution | $44 | $299 | $595 |
| **LTV:CAC (36-month)** | **0.26x** | **2.96x** | **9.0x** |
| 12-month LTV:CAC | 0.17x | 1.99x | 4.96x |
| **Payback (months, cohort average)** | **never** | **5** | **2** |

**Calibration check:** base-case month-6 retention (54%) falls inside the 45 to 62% vendor benchmark. Month-12 retention (35%) is consistent with ~7%/mo steady-state churn after early drop-off.

#### Sensitivity (base case, varying one input)
- **Steady-state churn × CAC** (LTV:CAC / payback months):

| Steady churn | CAC $100 | CAC $150 | CAC $200 | CAC $250 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.5% | 5.6x / 3 | 3.7x / 5 | 2.8x / 7 | 2.2x / 9 |
| 7.0% | 4.4x / 3 | 3.0x / 5 | 2.2x / 7 | 1.8x / 10 |
| 10.0% | 3.6x / 3 | 2.4x / 5 | 1.8x / 7 | 1.4x / 10 |

- **Price/mo** (CAC $150): $49 → 1.6x, 10-mo payback; $59 → 2.3x, 7 mo; $69 → 3.0x, 5 mo; $79 → 3.6x, 4 mo; $89 → 4.3x, 3 mo.
- **COGS + fulfillment/mo:** $12 → 3.4x; $18 → 3.0x; $24 → 2.5x; $30 → 2.1x. COGS matters less than price, CAC or churn.
- **Initial test price** (cost $85): free → 2.3x, 9-mo payback; $49 → 2.6x, 7 mo; $99 → 3.0x, 5 mo; $149 → 3.3x, 3 mo.

#### Variants
- **Quiz-only tier (no test):** $59/mo, $17 COGS, $110 CAC, churn 25% / 14% / 9%. Gives LTV ~$292, **2.7x**, 5-month payback, 26% active at month 12. Comparable to the lab tier on ratio, but with lower absolute LTV and weaker retention.
- **All-in membership:** $99/mo including the initial test and a month-13 retest (cost $85 each), $200 CAC, churn 22% / 12% / 6%. Gives LTV ~$714, **3.6x**, 6-month payback.

#### Interpretation
- **Vilo clears the 3:1 floor only if:**
  - fully loaded CAC per subscriber is ≤~$150; and
  - steady churn is ≤~7%; and
  - price is ≥~$69/mo; and
  - the test is priced at or near cost.
- **Base contribution covers only marketing.** At ~$45/mo, Vilo needs ~20 to 25k active subscribers (~$1.0 to 1.1M/month contribution) to cover a lean fixed-cost base of ~$12M/yr (fixed cost is an **estimate**: clinicians, engineering, formulation, G&A).
- **Retest loop as the retention mechanic:** if a month-3 to month-4 retest that visibly re-formulates the pack moves steady churn from 7% to 4.5%, LTV:CAC rises from 3.0x to 3.7x at $150 CAC. That is worth up to ~$100 per subscriber in lifetime contribution, so a retest costing ≤$85 roughly pays for itself in the base case. **This is a hypothesis to test**; no public data proves the effect.
- **Annual prepay** (e.g., $690/yr ≈ $57.50/mo) front-loads cash, giving payback inside month 1 on a cash basis, and caps first-year churn. Vendor data suggests 12-month NRR of ~96% vs ~70% for monthly billing.

### Gaps
- **Lab, physician and COGS figures are estimates** and must be cross-checked with the ops, lab and manufacturing researchers:
  - wholesale price of a women's panel (CBC, CMP, lipids, ferritin, vitamin D, B12, folate, TSH, HbA1c, hs-CRP, and any cycle-timed hormones);
  - physician order and review fee;
  - per-pack ingredient cost;
  - personalized sachet fulfillment cost;
  - USPS/UPS rates at Vilo's weight and zone.
- **No sourced data** on test-to-subscription conversion, retention lift from retesting, or real-world CAC for women's personalized supplements on TikTok or with influencers.
- **Payment-processing rate** assumed, not sourced in-session.
- **Not modeled:** refunds and chargebacks, sales tax, reformulation SKUs, inventory write-offs, HSA/FSA fees, and app-store fees (for any in-app purchase).

### Inferences
- **Investors are paying up for:**
  - recurring diagnostic memberships with AI layers (Function at ~25x run-rate revenue if the ~$100M Sacra estimate holds; not verified);
  - insurance-billed clinical women's care (Midi at ~6.7x run rate per [Multiples.vc](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/midi-health)).
  
  They are not paying up for supplement-first personalization. **For fundraising, Vilo should position as women's health data and diagnostics with a high-margin consumable, not as a "vitamin brand."**
- **Strategic CPG acquirers are retrenching from mass VMS** (Nestlé, Bayer) and keeping premium or practitioner brands (Solgar, Pure Encapsulations). This suggests a premium, clinician-reviewed positioning is the more acquirable profile. The likeliest acquirers or partners are:
  - platforms (Hims & Hers, Oura, Function, Superpower, Fullscript);
  - premium VMS owners;
  - not mass CPG.
