## 1. Dietary supplement law (DSHEA, claims, notifications, NDI, labeling of personalized packs, registration, adverse events, and FDA actions 2023-2026)

### Takeaway
Vilo's personalized formula is regulated as an ordinary dietary supplement. There is no premarket approval, but strict rules apply to claims, labels, ingredients, manufacturing practices and adverse-event reporting. The biggest supplement-law risk for a women's-health app is drifting into disease claims: PCOS, infertility, menopause "treatment", anemia or thyroid. A disease claim turns the product into an unapproved drug, and the app's own personalized recommendations count as labeling and advertising.

FDA policy under the current administration (2025-2026) has mostly moved toward deregulation and flexibility:
- NMN was reinstated as a lawful supplement ingredient in September 2025.
- FDA granted enforcement discretion on where the DSHEA disclaimer must appear on the label (December 2025).
- FDA held a March 2026 meeting that could widen what counts as a "dietary ingredient".

Mandatory product listing is still only a bill and has not been enacted.

### Cited Findings

**DSHEA basics and claims**
- A dietary supplement label may carry a "structure/function" statement under FD&C Act §403(r)(6) without FDA approval. The conditions are:
  - the claim must be truthful and substantiated;
  - the label must carry the DSHEA disclaimer ("This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.");
  - FDA must be notified no later than 30 days after first marketing the claim.

  Sources: 21 U.S.C. §343(r)(6) — [LII 21 U.S.C. 343](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/343); 21 CFR 101.93 — [eCFR 21 CFR 101.93](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-101/subpart-F/section-101.93). Primary text, not fetched this session; counsel to confirm.
- §403(r)(6)(A) also permits a statement that claims a benefit related to a *classical nutrient deficiency disease* (for example scurvy or rickets), but only if the label discloses how common that disease is in the US. This is the narrow statutory path for "deficiency" language. Source: [LII 21 U.S.C. 343](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/343) (not fetched; counsel to confirm how it applies to "iron deficiency"-type messaging).
- **DSHEA disclaimer placement, December 2025.**
  - FDA issued a letter to industry saying it will exercise enforcement discretion over the requirement that the disclaimer appear on *every label panel* carrying a structure/function claim, while it considers amending 21 CFR 101.93(d).
  - Discretion does **not** cover the requirement to have the disclaimer on the label at all, or the requirement to link each claim to it (usually with an asterisk).
  - Sources: [FDA letter to industry](https://www.fda.gov/food/information-industry-dietary-supplements/letter-dietary-supplement-industry-dshea-disclaimer); [NutraIngredients, Dec 11 2025](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/12/11/fda-considers-rulemaking-and-enforcement-discretion-on-dshea-disclaimer); [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/fda-signals-flexibility-on-placement-of-the-dshea-disclaimer).
- **CRN citizen petition, May 6, 2026.** The Council for Responsible Nutrition asked FDA to confirm that a single, prominent boxed disclaimer on one panel complies. It also asked that this apply retroactively and preempt private class actions over disclaimer placement. That is CRN's request, not an FDA commitment, and no proposed or final rule was found as of this research. Sources: [CRN petition PDF](https://www.crnusa.org/sites/default/files/2026/2026.05.06%20CRN%20Citizen%20Petition.pdf); [Nutritional Outlook](https://www.nutritionaloutlook.com/view/crn-petitions-fda-dshea-disclaimer-placement-supplement-labels).
- Class actions over disclaimer placement have increased, according to CRN, and law firms treat FDA's letter as a possible but untested litigation defense. Source: [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/fda-signals-flexibility-on-placement-of-the-dshea-disclaimer).

**Women's-health claim precedents**
- FDA's February 18, 2021 warning letter to FDC Nutrition treated an inositol product as an unapproved drug and misbranded under §502(f)(1). The trigger was a label claim that it would improve fertility in women with PCOS. Source: [FDA warning letter, FDC Nutrition](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/fdc-nutrition-inc-612264-02182021).
- A joint FDA/FTC warning letter to a fertility-supplement seller objected to claims about luteal phase defect, PCOS, IVF and Clomid users. Source: [FDA/FTC letter, ConceiveMedia](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/warning-letters/fda-conceivemedia.pdf).
- Industry counsel's rule of thumb: "normal symptomology" of life stages is permissible for women's-health products, but "abnormal conditions" are not. This is commentary, not FDA text. Source: [NutraIngredients, Apr 2023](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2023/04/20/Women-s-health-claims-What-you-can-and-cannot-say/).
- In October 2024 Truth in Advertising (TINA.org) sent letters to several menopause-supplement companies over hot-flash and symptom claims. Source: [TINA.org letters](https://truthinadvertising.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/10_7_24-Notification-Letters-to-Menopause-Supplement-Companies_Combined.pdf).

**New Dietary Ingredients (NDI)**
- An ingredient not marketed in the US before October 15, 1994 is a "new dietary ingredient". It needs a premarket NDI notification unless it has been present in the food supply as an article used for food in a form not chemically altered. Statute: [LII 21 U.S.C. 350b](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/350b) (not fetched).
- The NDI notification must be filed at least 75 days before the ingredient enters interstate commerce. Source: [Orionex Regulatory, secondary](https://www.orionexregulatory.com/supplement-manufacturer-fda-registration).
- **NMN.**
  - In two letters dated September 29, 2025, FDA reversed its 2022 position. It concluded that nicotinamide mononucleotide is *not* excluded from the dietary-supplement definition, because evidence showed it was marketed as a supplement by 2017, before it was authorized for investigation as a new drug.
  - In December 2025 FDA sent letters to ingredient firms reinstating their NMN NDI status.
  - CRN criticized the decision for leaving the structural "drug preclusion" problem unresolved.
  - Sources: [Venable, Oct 2025](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/10/fda-declares-nicotinamide-mononucleotide-is); [NutraIngredients, Sept 30 2025](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/09/30/fda-declares-nmn-lawful-in-dietary-supplements/); [NutraIngredients, Dec 9 2025](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/12/09/fda-reinstates-ndi-status-of-nmn-with-new-letters-to-ingredient-players); [SupplySide SJ](https://www.supplysidesj.com/supplement-regulations/fda-s-nmn-decision-sparks-mixed-reactions-from-supplement-industry-raises-questions).
  - Venable notes plaintiffs may still challenge NMN products under California's Unfair Competition Law. Source: [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/10/fda-declares-nicotinamide-mononucleotide-is).
- **"Dietary ingredient" scope meeting, March 27, 2026.**
  - FDA's Office of Dietary Supplement Programs held a public meeting, "Exploring the Scope of Dietary Supplement Ingredients" (docket FDA-2026-N-2047; comments were due April 27, 2026).
  - Topics: what counts as a "dietary substance"; whether synthesis, cell culture, precision fermentation and recombinant production change an ingredient's identity; and identity criteria for proteins, enzymes and microbials.
  - No outcome had been reported in the sources found.
  - Sources: [Olsson Frank Weeda](https://ofwlaw.com/?p=13597); [Alston & Bird, Apr 14 2026](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/fda-modernize-dietary-supplement-regulation); [SBA Office of Advocacy](https://advocacy.sba.gov/2026/03/31/fda-seeks-comments-on-the-scope-of-dietary-supplement-ingredients/); [WholeFoods Magazine](https://www.wholefoodsmagazine.com/articles/17994-industry-pushes-for-clarity-as-fda-reassesses-dietary-substance-definition-in-dshea).

**Labeling: the Supplement Facts panel, and how it applies to per-customer packs**
- Required label elements:
  - statement of identity;
  - net contents;
  - Supplement Facts panel (21 CFR 101.36);
  - ingredient list (21 CFR 101.4);
  - name and place of business of the manufacturer, packer or distributor;
  - plus the domestic address or phone number required for adverse-event reporting (see below).

  Sources: [eCFR 21 CFR 101.36](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-101/subpart-C/section-101.36) (not fetched); [YLT Packing, secondary summary](https://yltpacking.com/dietary-supplement-packaging-containers-labels-fda-rules/); [CHPA labeling overview](https://www.chpa.org/public-policy-regulatory/regulation/regulation-dietary-supplements/labeling-dietary-supplements).
- FDA's Dietary Supplement Labeling Guide (Chapter IV) says individual units in a multi-unit retail package may omit the Supplement Facts panel. The conditions are that the outer package carries full nutrition labeling and the units are not intended for separate retail sale. Source: [FDA Labeling Guide Ch. IV](https://www.fda.gov/food/dietary-supplements-guidance-documents-regulatory-information/dietary-supplement-labeling-guide-chapter-iv-nutrition-labeling), via search summary; the page itself could not be fetched.

**Facility registration, cGMP and adverse-event reporting**
- Facility registration: every facility that manufactures, processes, packs or holds food (including supplements) for US consumption must register with FDA under the Bioterrorism Act and renew in each even-numbered year (21 U.S.C. §350d). Source: [LII 21 U.S.C. 350d](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/350d) (not fetched). This applies to Vilo if it packs or holds the product itself, not only to its contract manufacturer.
- Supplement cGMP rules are in 21 CFR Part 111. Source: [eCFR 21 CFR Part 111](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-111) (not fetched). Manufacturing operations are out of scope for these notes.
- Mandatory serious adverse event reporting under the Dietary Supplement and Nonprescription Drug Consumer Protection Act (2006), 21 U.S.C. §379aa-1:
  - the firm whose name appears on the label must report serious adverse events to FDA (MedWatch 3500A) within 15 business days;
  - it must keep records for 6 years;
  - the label must carry a domestic address or phone number for receiving reports.

  Source: [LII 21 U.S.C. 379aa-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/379aa-1) (not fetched; counsel to confirm details).

**Mandatory product listing (pending legislation, not law)**
- The Dietary Supplement Listing Act of 2026 was introduced as S.3677 (Sen. Durbin, January 2026) and H.R. 8370 (Rep. Maxine Dexter, April 20, 2026). Earlier versions failed in 2022 and 2024.
- Under the House text, listing numbers would have to appear on labels starting two years after enactment. The bill expressly bars FDA from treating listing as premarket approval.
- CRN supports the bill; the Natural Products Association (NPA) opposes it. It had not been enacted as of these sources.
- Sources: [NutraIngredients, Jan 15 2026](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/01/15/sen-durbin-introduces-dietary-supplement-listing-act-of-2026/); [H.R. 8370 text](https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr8370/BILLS-119hr8370ih.pdf); [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/is-your-product-ready-to-be-listed-what-the-dietary-supplement-listing-act-of-2026-means-for-your-business); [WholeFoods Magazine](https://www.wholefoodsmagazine.com/articles/17993-house-dietary-supplement-listing-act-sparks-debate-over-fda-product-registry).

**MAHA and FDA leadership context**
- The MAHA Strategy Report (2025) said little about supplements. It promised to study "potential health benefits of select high-quality supplements" and warned that FDA will act against unapproved products marketed as supplements. Source: [Nutraceuticals World](https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/how-do-supplements-fit-into-the-maha-strategy/).
- HHS Secretary Kennedy has pledged to "end the war at FDA" on peptides and supplements. In July 2026 an FDA advisory panel voted, against FDA scientists' assessment, to ease compounding restrictions on six peptides including BPC-157 and TB-500. This concerns **compounding (the 503A list)**, not supplement status. Sources: [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/health/fda-to-weigh-easing-limits-on-unproven-peptides-favored-by-rfk-jr-and-maha-supporters); [CNN, Oct 13 2026](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/13/health/peptides-maha-access); [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/healthcare/5832457-fda-considers-peptide-access/).
- Critics describe the December 2025 disclaimer letter as weakening the "quack Miranda warning". This is opinion. Source: [Science-Based Medicine](https://sciencebasedmedicine.org/fda-under-maha-control/).

### Inferences
- **Each personalized pack is a labeled supplement.**
  - Each shipment needs a compliant label: a Supplement Facts panel reflecting the *exact per-serving amounts in that customer's formula*, plus the ingredient list, disclaimer linkage, net quantity, distributor and AER contact.
  - Practical approaches: (a) print a variable-data Supplement Facts panel on the outer box or pouch for each customer, with daily sachets as non-retail inner units (relying on the multi-unit exemption); or (b) print a full panel on every sachet.
  - Option (a) appears to fit the FDA multi-unit language only if sachets are not sold separately. Counsel to confirm.
  - If Vilo blends one capsule or powder per user (rather than combining pre-made SKUs), its "formula" is a new product per customer. Label-generation software becomes a regulated labeling system and needs QA, version control and record retention.
- **App messages count as labeling or advertising.** Personalized outputs ("Your ferritin is low, so we added iron to support energy") are likely to be treated as labeling or advertising. They must stay within structure/function framing:
  - Allowed framing: "supports healthy energy levels", "supports normal menstrual comfort".
  - Not allowed: "treats iron-deficiency anemia", "for PCOS", "improves fertility", "relieves menopause" (disease-like).
  - The disclaimer and the asterisk linkage should also appear wherever the app or website makes structure/function claims.
- **30-day notification.** One FDA 30-day notification can cover the claim library. Vilo should keep a finite, pre-approved claim library rather than let a generative engine produce new claims. New claims mean new notifications and new substantiation.
- **Ingredient selection.** Use only ingredients with clear pre-1994 or NDI-notified status. Avoid peptides, NMN-type contested ingredients, "drug-preclusion" ingredients and hormones or hormone precursors. The status quo is in flux (NMN reversal, the March 2026 meeting, peptide politics), and contested ingredients bring extra class-action and FDA risk to a women's-health brand.
- **Deregulatory climate.** It lowers FDA enforcement risk at the margin. It does not reduce FTC, state AG or private class-action risk, which have become the main enforcement channels.

No primary or secondary source was found. Recommend buying a Persona box and reviewing the label, and asking regulatory counsel and contract packers.
- FDA labeling-guide text could not be fetched verbatim (fda.gov blocked). Labeling of small packages and strip packs in particular (21 CFR 101.36(i)) is unverified.

Counsel should confirm current applicability.

## 2. FTC advertising law: substantiation, "personalized" and "doctor-designed" claims, endorsements and reviews, and subscriptions (click-to-cancel and state auto-renewal laws)

### Takeaway
The FTC (with state AGs and class-action lawyers) is the main US enforcer against supplement marketing. It requires "competent and reliable scientific evidence" for health claims, which for health benefits generally means human RCTs on the actual formulation or a closely matched one. The closest precedent for Vilo is the 2014 FTC case against GeneLink/foru over "genetically customized" supplements. That order covered both unsupported personalization and health claims *and* data-security failures.

The federal click-to-cancel rule remains vacated (Eighth Circuit, July 2025). The FTC restarted rulemaking with an ANPRM on March 11, 2026, and keeps enforcing ROSCA and Section 5. California's amended auto-renewal law (in force since July 1, 2025) is the practical compliance baseline.

### Cited Findings

**Substantiation standard**
- The FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (December 2022) replaced the 1998 dietary-supplement guide and covers all health-related products. Health claims require "competent and reliable scientific evidence", and advertisers must have substantiation for express and implied claims *before* dissemination. Sources: [FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (PDF)](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Health-Products-Compliance-Guidance.pdf); [FTC Health Claims hub](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/truth-advertising/health-claims).
- Several secondary sources read the Guidance as generally requiring RCTs for supplement efficacy claims. Treat this as a practitioner interpretation of the Guidance. Source: [search summary of FTC guidance and practitioner commentary](https://www.foodchainid.com/resources/united-states-updated-ftc-guidance-on-claims-in-advertising/).

**Personalization precedent (GeneLink/foru)**
- GeneLink and foru International sold supplements "genetically customized" from cheek-swab DNA for at least $100 per month to about 30,000 consumers. The FTC alleged:
  - unsupported claims that customization compensates for genetic disadvantages;
  - testimonial-based disease claims (diabetes, heart disease, insomnia);
  - inadequate data security.
- Final consent orders (May 12, 2014):
  - bar disease claims unless validated by well-controlled studies;
  - require a comprehensive security program with biennial audits for 20 years.
- Sources: [FTC press release, Jan 2014](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2014/01/companies-pitching-genetically-customized-nutritional-supplements); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/ftc-approves-consent-orders-companies-marketed-genetically-customized-nutritional-supplements); [Kelley Drye Ad Law Access](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ftc-announces-settlement-with-makers-of-genetically-customized-dietary-supplements-and-skin-serums).

**Recent supplement actions (2026)**
- FTC v. Amare Global: the FTC moved for contempt (filed June 12, 2026) against the company and three individuals over unsubstantiated mental-health claims for children and adults. Commentary flags brand liability for claims made by distributors and partners. Sources: [FTC Health Claims hub](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/truth-advertising/health-claims); [AuditSocials commentary, secondary](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/ftc-amare-global-2026-supplement-mental-health-claims-children-brand-partner-liability).
- TruHeight (April 2026): FTC action over unsubstantiated height-growth supplement claims aimed at kids and teens. Source: [FTC Health Claims hub](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/truth-advertising/health-claims).

**Endorsements and reviews**
- The FTC Endorsement Guides were last updated in 2023 and remain the source of influencer disclosure duties. The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (fully in effect October 2024) targets fake, AI-generated or misattributed reviews, review suppression, and incentives conditioned on positive sentiment.
- On December 22, 2025 the FTC sent its first warning letters under the Rule to 10 unnamed companies. Commentary notes that supplements, health and wellness, and beauty are especially exposed. Civil penalties are available under the Rule.
- Sources: [Venable, Dec 2025](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-signals-heightened-enforcement-of-new); [Arnold & Porter, Jan 2026](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/consumer-products-and-retail-navigator/2026/01/ftc-warning-letters-over-consumer-review-rule); [FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2026/01/07/ftc-signals-crackdown-on-fake-reviews-and-testimonials); [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/united-states/federal-trade-commission/ftc-issues-warning-letters-for-violations-of-consumer-reviews-rule/).

**Click-to-cancel and ROSCA**
- The Eighth Circuit vacated the FTC's 2024 "click-to-cancel" amendments to the Negative Option Rule in July 2025, for failure to follow Magnuson-Moss procedure (no preliminary regulatory analysis). Source: [Gibson Dunn](https://gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/).
- On March 11, 2026 the FTC issued an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM) on amending the Negative Option Rule. It is unclear whether a new rule will match the vacated one, and no NPRM was found in these sources (as of May 2026). Sources: [Greenberg Traurig, Mar 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/ar/insights/2026/3/ftc-seeks-comment-on-potential-updates-to-negative-option-rule); [Jones Day, May 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/05/ftc-revives-clicktocancel-rule-new-risks-for-subscription-businesses); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/clicking-all-the-right-boxes-ftc-moves-to-revive-click-to-cancel-rule-following-eighth-circuit-vacatur).
- The FTC continues to enforce ROSCA and Section 5. In its NextMed complaint (a telehealth/weight-loss seller) it pleaded the vacated rule's requirements as Section 5 violations. About 30 states have their own auto-renewal or negative-option laws. Sources: [Gibson Dunn](https://gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/); [Arnold & Porter, Feb 2026](https://arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/02/ftc-and-state-ags-continue-to-scrutinize-subscription-practices); [Goodwin, Feb 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/insights/publications/2026/02/alerts-practices-ba-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-gets-new-life).

**California auto-renewal law (AB 2863)**
- Signed September 24, 2024; effective for contracts entered into, amended or extended on or after July 1, 2025. It covers free-to-pay conversions and requires:
  - express affirmative consent to the auto-renewal terms (B&P §16702(a)(4));
  - annual reminders;
  - online "click to cancel" in the same medium as sign-up;
  - no misrepresentation of material terms.
- Sources: [Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect); [Fenwick](https://fenwick.com/insights/publications/california-tightens-requirements-for-automatically-renewing-subscriptions); [DWT](https://dwt.com/insights/2024/10/ab-2863-updates-california-automatic-renewal-law); [Kilpatrick Townsend](https://ktslaw.com/en/insights/alert/2024/10/california-latest-automatic-renewal-law-amendments-take-effect-in-july-2025).

### Inferences
- **"Personalized" is an objective claim that needs substantiation.**
  - Claims that imply a measurable outcome ("formulated for *your* biology", "optimized from your bloodwork to fix your fatigue", "better results than generic multivitamins") require evidence that the personalization algorithm improves outcomes compared with non-personalized supplementation.
  - GeneLink shows the FTC will test the personalization premise itself.
  - Safer framing describes process, not outcome: "your formula is selected based on your answers and lab values, using published dietary reference intakes".
- **"Clinically formulated", "doctor-designed" and "clinically proven" are implied-efficacy and expert-endorsement claims.**
  - "Doctor-designed" or "OB-GYN-formulated" requires that qualified physicians actually did the formulation and that their expertise was exercised. Under the Endorsement Guides, an expert endorsement must reflect the expert's genuine evaluation.
  - "Clinically proven" requires RCTs on the formula or a materially equivalent one.
  - "Clinically studied ingredients" invites a misleading net impression if the doses or populations differ from the studies.
- **Testimonials.** A women's-health brand is at high risk of testimonial-driven disease claims ("cured my PCOS", "got pregnant after 3 months"). Under the Endorsement Guides, Vilo is liable for implied claims in testimonials and influencer content it uses. Vilo should:
  - moderate reviews, without suppressing negative ones (the Review Rule bans suppression);
  - avoid incentivizing positive reviews;
  - require #ad-type disclosures;
  - monitor affiliates.
- **Subscriptions.** Design to California AB 2863 plus ROSCA:
  - clear disclosure of price, frequency and how to cancel before billing data is collected;
  - a separate affirmative consent checkbox;
  - an immediate confirmation email;
  - online one-click cancellation without forced retention offers;
  - reminders before free-to-paid conversion and annually;
  - price-change notices.

  Following California's rules generally satisfies most of the roughly 30 state auto-renewal laws (counsel to confirm the outliers, for example NY, CO and MN).

### Gaps
- Not verified this session (background knowledge, verify): the Endorsement Guides revision was announced June 29, 2023; the Consumer Review Rule took effect October 21, 2024; civil penalties are about $53,000 per violation (inflation-adjusted annually).
- The April 2023 FTC Notices of Penalty Offenses on substantiation (sent to roughly 670 health-product marketers) could not be verified because the search budget ran out. If confirmed, they put recipients on notice and enable civil penalties.
- No FTC case specifically against a supplement *subscription* business in 2025-2026 was found. The NextMed and other ROSCA actions are the nearest analogs.

## 3. Is the recommendation engine a medical device? (General Wellness 2026, CDS 2026, interpreting labs and dosing, and how competitors handle it)

### Takeaway
On January 6, 2026 FDA issued revised final General Wellness and Clinical Decision Support (CDS) guidances. Both loosen the rules, but neither clearly covers *patient-facing software that interprets a user's lab values to identify a deficiency or condition and recommends a "treatment" dose*. That is the riskiest design. The CDS exemption in the statute is limited to software intended for healthcare professionals (HCPs). Vilo stays furthest from device territory if:
- outputs are framed as general wellness and nutrition-intake optimization ("supports healthy iron levels"), without naming conditions or using diagnostic thresholds; and/or
- any interpretation of clinical lab values is done by, or presented as reviewed by, a licensed clinician (an HCP-facing CDS model).

The 2024 FDA rule treating laboratory-developed tests (LDTs) as devices was vacated in 2025, which lowers lab-side device risk.

### Cited Findings

**January 6, 2026 guidances**
- FDA issued revised final General Wellness (superseding 2019) and CDS (superseding 2022) guidances on January 6, 2026, without prior public comment. Sources: [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/fda-updates-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-guidance-documents); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/01/fda-cuts-red-tape-on-clinical-decision-support-software); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-it/insights/publications/2026/01/fda-updates-its-clinical-decision-support-and-general-wellness-guidances-key-points); [FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/?p=15814).
- General Wellness 2026, scope:
  - The two-factor test is unchanged: the product must be intended only for general wellness use and present low risk.
  - The guidance now allows non-invasive wearables that measure physiologic parameters, including blood pressure and blood glucose, to be wellness products when marketed for wellness.
  - Invasive or riskier products (implants, lasers, radiation) remain outside.
  - Sources: [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/01/fda-adapts-with-the-times-on-digital-health-updated-guidances-on-general-wellness-products); [Faegre Drinker](https://faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/1/key-updates-in-fdas-2026-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-software-guidance); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-20-fda-opens-aperture-for-wearables-in-latest-general-wellness-guidance).
- General Wellness 2026, what a wellness product may and may not say:
  - It may tell users that evaluation by an HCP may be recommended when outputs fall outside wellness ranges.
  - It may not name a condition, call a value "abnormal", give clinical thresholds or treatment advice, or provide ongoing alerts for disease management.
  - FDA has not said what range counts as "appropriate for general wellness".
  - Sources: [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/fda-guidance-eases-wearables-oversight-but-experts-have-questions-about-wh/810581/); [Hardian Health](https://www.hardianhealth.com/insights/fda-wearables-guidelines-update-2026); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-20-fda-opens-aperture-for-wearables-in-latest-general-wellness-guidance).
- CDS 2026 changes:
  - FDA no longer automatically treats software supporting an HCP's *time-critical* decisions as a device.
  - FDA will exercise enforcement discretion for software that offers a single recommendation, if it otherwise meets the §520(o)(1)(E) criteria.
  - FDA expects clear, accessible documentation for HCPs on how the software reaches its recommendations.
  - Neither guidance expressly changes FDA's approach to AI.
  - Sources: [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/01/fda-cuts-red-tape-on-clinical-decision-support-software); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/de/insights/2026/01/a-relaxing-2026-fda-updates-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-software-guidance); [Faegre Drinker](https://faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/1/key-updates-in-fdas-2026-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-software-guidance).
- Statutory software exclusions (21st Century Cures Act, FD&C §520(o)):
  - §520(o)(1)(B) excludes software for "maintaining or encouraging a healthy lifestyle" that is *unrelated to the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, prevention, or treatment of a disease or condition*.
  - §520(o)(1)(E) (the CDS exclusion) applies only to software that supports or provides recommendations *to a health care professional*, where the HCP can independently review the basis. It does not apply to software that analyzes a medical image or a signal from an in vitro diagnostic.
  - Source: [LII 21 U.S.C. 360j](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/360j) (not fetched; counsel to confirm).

**Lab-developed tests**
- FDA's 2024 rule regulating LDTs as devices was vacated by a federal district court on March 31, 2025. FDA formally rescinded it on September 18, 2025. Source: [AHA News](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2025-09-18-fda-vacates-final-rule-regulating-lab-developed-tests-medical-devices).

**Competitors**
- InsideTracker uses CLIA-certified labs, and a licensed physician reviews and authorizes each lab requisition. The service is described as not FDA-cleared as a diagnostic. One source conflicts and claims FDA clearance; that claim is unverified and likely wrong. Source: [HealthRx, secondary](https://healthrx.com/brands-inside-tracker/prescription-process).

### Inferences
- **Device-risk spectrum, lowest to highest:**
  1. Questionnaire and intake-based nutrient recommendations against dietary reference intakes. This is low risk, a general wellness or lifestyle function.
  2. Wearable sleep, HRV and cycle data used for "wellness" tips. Low risk under the 2026 guidance, if no disease naming.
  3. Displaying lab values with population reference ranges and general nutrition education. Moderate risk.
  4. **An algorithm that flags "deficiency" or "insufficiency" from lab values and automatically sets a "corrective" dose shown to the consumer. This is high risk.** It analyzes IVD results to identify a condition and recommends treatment, and it is patient-facing, so it cannot use the CDS exclusion.
  5. Anything naming or screening for conditions (PCOS, hypothyroidism, anemia, perimenopause staging, fertility windows for contraception). These are device functions; for example, contraception apps such as Natural Cycles are FDA-cleared devices.
- **Mitigation options:**
  - Keep dosing within general-population safe ranges (at or below the tolerable upper intake level, UL) and frame lab inputs as "personalization signals", not diagnoses.
  - Route any out-of-range lab value to a "talk to your clinician" message, which the 2026 wellness guidance appears to tolerate, rather than a supplement "fix".
  - Or build an HCP-in-the-loop model, where a licensed clinician reviews labs and approves recommendations, and the software is HCP-facing CDS meeting all four criteria. This ties into practice-of-medicine issues (Section 5).
- **Claims drive both device status and drug status.** The same claim discipline protects against both device classification (software intended use) and drug classification (supplement intended use).
- **Fertile-window and ovulation prediction.** If Vilo shows fertile windows, it must avoid any contraception or conception-aid intended use, which would be a regulated device, unless it obtains clearance.

- The full text of the 2026 General Wellness and CDS guidances was not fetched (fda.gov blocked). Whether they address interpretation of consumer-initiated lab tests or supplement or nutrition recommendations is unverified.
- No source found on how Function Health or Persona structure their FDA position.
- Whether FDA treats discrete lab results as "medical information" or as an IVD "signal" under CDS criterion 1 is unverified. The researcher's recollection is that the 2022 guidance treated lab results as medical information; counsel to confirm against the 2026 text.

## 4. Privacy and security: HIPAA, the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, state consumer-health-data laws, comprehensive state laws, reproductive data after Dobbs, and best practices

### Takeaway
Vilo is very likely **not** a HIPAA covered entity. Instead it sits in a stricter, litigation-heavy patchwork:
- the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (HBNR, amended effective July 29, 2024), under which unauthorized sharing with ad platforms is a "breach";
- FTC Act §5 unfairness;
- Washington's My Health My Data Act (MHMDA, with a private right of action);
- Nevada SB 370 and Connecticut's consumer-health-data provisions;
- Maryland MODPA (bans the *sale* of sensitive data even with consent; applies from April 1, 2026);
- California CMIA (applies to reproductive and sexual health apps via AB 254) and CIPA wiretap claims. The Flo Health case produced an August 2025 jury verdict against Meta.

The 2024 HIPAA reproductive-health rule was vacated nationwide on June 18, 2025, and would not have covered a direct-to-consumer (DTC) app in any case. Menstrual and reproductive data from a DTC app is therefore reachable by subpoena or warrant unless Vilo designs to minimize what it holds. New York's revised Health Information Privacy Act passed both houses in June 2026 and was awaiting the governor's action as of the latest sources.

### Cited Findings

**HIPAA**
- HHS OCR's Health App Use Scenarios guidance (February 2016):
  - An app developer is a business associate only when it creates, receives, maintains or transmits PHI on behalf of a covered entity or business associate; in practice, when it is hired or paid by one and directed to offer the app.
  - A consumer choosing to send data to a provider does not make the developer a BA, even if the provider recommended the app.
  - A developer can be a BA for a plan- or provider-offered version while its separate DTC app is outside HIPAA, if the data is kept separate.
  - A provider contracting with a developer for patient management (messaging, monitoring, counseling) makes the developer a BA.
  - Sources: [HHS OCR scenarios PDF](https://hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr-health-app-developer-scenarios-2-2016.pdf); [HHS Health Apps page](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-apps/); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/health-apps-and-hipaa-ocr-publishes-new-guidance-for-health-app-developers).
- A "covered entity" is a health plan, a clearinghouse, or a healthcare provider that conducts HIPAA standard electronic transactions (for example insurance claims). Source: [eCFR 45 CFR 160.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-160/subpart-A/section-160.103) (not fetched).
- **HIPAA reproductive-health rule.** On June 18, 2025, Judge Kacsmaryk (N.D. Tex., *Purl v. HHS*) vacated nationwide nearly all of the 2024 HIPAA Privacy Rule amendments protecting reproductive health PHI. Some notice-of-privacy-practices changes tied to substance use disorder (Part 2) survived. No appeal or replacement rulemaking was found. Sources: [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/federal-court-vacates-2024-hipaa-reproductive-health-privacy-rule/); [Husch Blackwell](https://huschblackwell.com/newsandinsights/federal-court-vacates-2024-hipaa-reproductive-health-privacy-rule-key-impacts-for-covered-entities-and-npp-compliance); [UNC School of Government](https://www.sog.unc.edu/blogs/coates-canons/final-update-2024-hipaa-final-rule-regarding-reproductive-health-0); [Quarles](https://www.quarles.com/newsroom/publications/hipaa-reproductive-health-rule-vacated-nationally).

**FTC Health Breach Notification Rule**
- Final rule issued April 26, 2024, published May 30, 2024, effective July 29, 2024.
- It clarifies that most health apps are "vendors of personal health records". It codifies the FTC's September 2021 policy statement and the 2023 GoodRx and Easy Healthcare (Premom) cases: a "breach of security" includes *unauthorized disclosures* (for example to ad platforms), not just hacks.
- Sources: [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/30/2024-10855/health-breach-notification-rule); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/240514-ftc-issues-final-rule-to-expand); [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2024/05/ftc-finalizes-hbnr-to-cover-health-app-breaches); [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/zh/insights/publications/2024/7/ftc-updates-health-breach-notification-rule); [Debevoise](https://debevoise.com/insights/publications/2024/05/federal-trade-commission-finalizes-updates).
- Related FTC cases:
  - GoodRx paid a $1.5 million civil penalty (the first HBNR case).
  - Premom, GoodRx and BetterHelp were each penalized for sharing health information with third parties without affirmative express consent.
  - Premom's order bans sharing health data for advertising and requires consent for other sharing.
  - Sources: [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/08/ftc-updated-health-breach-notification-rule); [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/zh/insights/publications/2024/7/ftc-updates-health-breach-notification-rule).

**Flo Health litigation (*Frasco v. Flo Health*, N.D. Cal. 3:21-cv-00757)**

Google, Flo and Flurry settled for a combined $59.5 million: Google $48M, Flo $8M, Flurry $3.5M (approval pending at the time of that report).

**Nevada, Connecticut and California**

**Maryland MODPA**

### Inferences
- **HIPAA status.** Vilo is not a HIPAA covered entity if it is cash-pay DTC and does not bill insurance electronically. If it contracts with a physician network or medical group that orders labs, and handles results *on that group's behalf*, it may be a business associate for that function. Mixed status is common and should be deliberately structured: a BAA with the medical group, segregated PHI, and a clear consumer consent to move data into the Vilo consumer app. Being outside HIPAA means **FTC, HBNR and state laws apply in full**. It also means reproductive data has no HIPAA shield against subpoenas.
- **Ad-tech is the single biggest privacy liability.** GoodRx, BetterHelp, Premom and Flo were all about pixels and SDKs (Meta, Google, Flurry and similar) sending health or cycle data to advertisers. For Vilo:
  - no third-party advertising pixels or SDKs on logged-in, quiz, results or checkout screens;
  - no "custom audiences" built from health inputs;
  - server-side analytics with a strict event allowlist;
  - vendor contracts barring secondary use.

  CIPA statutory damages ($5,000 per violation) plus MHMDA's private right of action make class-action exposure potentially existential.
- **Consent architecture (to satisfy the strictest laws together: MHMDA, Nevada, Connecticut, Maryland, CCPA/CPRA "sensitive PI" and NY HIPA if enacted):**
  - separate, specific, unbundled opt-in consent for collecting health data and for each sharing purpose;
  - no sale of health data at all (Maryland bans it outright);
  - collect only what is strictly necessary for the requested formula;
  - a dedicated Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy, required by MHMDA and Nevada;
  - honor deletion and access rights;
  - treat *inferences* (for example "likely perimenopausal") as sensitive health data.
- **Reproductive-data hardening:**
  - store cycle logs on-device or end-to-end encrypted, so Vilo cannot read them;
  - set short retention periods;
  - avoid precise geolocation;
  - publish a law-enforcement request policy that requires a warrant and notifies the user where lawful, plus a transparency report;
  - avoid fields that imply pregnancy outcomes, abortion or miscarriage unless essential;
  - consider an "anonymous mode".

  These are design choices, not legal mandates (outside CA AB 254 and the CMIA), but they reduce harm and litigation exposure.
- **Breach response.** HBNR-covered breaches require notice to individuals, the FTC and sometimes the media. Unauthorized sharing counts as a breach, so a misconfigured pixel can trigger notification duties.
- **NY HIPA.** If signed, it would likely be the strictest health-data law for apps serving New Yorkers. Build to it now to avoid re-architecture.

## 5. Lab testing and medical-practice adjacencies: corporate practice of medicine, physician ordering, and dietitian licensure

### Takeaway
DTC lab testing usually requires a licensed clinician to authorize the order (InsideTracker's model, for example). This creates corporate practice of medicine (CPOM) structuring needs where Vilo works with physicians. Separately, *individualized* nutrition advice is regulated by a state patchwork, ranging from title protection only to exclusive-scope licensure. A small number of states restrict individualized nutrition counseling to licensed dietitians. Automated, personalized supplement recommendations are a grey zone. Lab operations are covered by another researcher.

### Cited Findings
- There is no nationwide law on who may give nutrition advice. States follow three models: title protection, licensure or certification, and exclusive scope of practice. Source: [state-statute summary (Shook, Hardy & Bacon / Grippo & Elden)](https://www.shb.com/-/media/files/professionals/d/dammrichthomas/summaryofstatestatutesgoverningtheprovisionofnutritionaleducationandadvice.pdf?la=en).
- **California** restricts only specific titles. Its statute expressly allows general nutrition advice, defined to include information on the use and role of food and food ingredients, *including dietary supplements*. **Washington** and **Colorado** do not restrict who provides nutrition advice. Sources: [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, California](https://www.eatrightpro.org/advocacy/licensure/licensure-map-and-statutes-by-state/california); [Shook Hardy summary](https://www.shb.com/-/media/files/professionals/d/dammrichthomas/summaryofstatestatutesgoverningtheprovisionofnutritionaleducationandadvice.pdf?la=en).
- **Nebraska** exempts individualized nutrition information and recommendations that do not amount to medical nutrition therapy (MNT). **Missouri** (2021) lets certified nutrition specialists practice everything except MNT, with disclosures. A November 2024 CNS advocacy chart lists **Alabama, Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota and Tennessee** as states where CNSs cannot provide individualized nutrition counseling. Sources: [Nebraska Rev. Stat. 38-1812](https://law.justia.com/codes/nebraska/chapter-38/statute-38-1812/); [CNS State-by-State chart, Nov 2024](https://www.theana.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/CNS-State-By-State-Practie-Rights_Updated-Nov-2024-1.pdf).
- Legislative activity in 2025-2026 includes a Massachusetts dietitian licensure modernization bill (H.453) and an Oklahoma Dietitian Licensure Compact bill (SB 805). Sources: [MA H.453](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H453.Html); [OK SB805 committee substitute](https://oklegislature.gov/cf_pdf/2025-26%20COMMITTEE%20SUBS/HCSS/SB805%20POLREC.PDF).
- InsideTracker's model has a licensed physician review and authorize every lab requisition, with no ongoing clinical relationship. Source: [HealthRx, secondary](https://healthrx.com/brands-inside-tracker/prescription-process).

### Inferences
- **CPOM.** In CPOM states (for example California, New York, Texas and New Jersey; counsel to confirm the list), a lay company like Vilo cannot employ physicians or control medical judgment. The usual structure is a friendly physician-owned professional corporation plus a management services organization (MSO) agreement, or an independent telehealth-physician network vendor. Lab ordering, result review and any clinician-signed recommendation should sit with the professional entity. Fees must be fair-market-value, not tied to supplement sales.
- **Physician involvement.** If physicians "approve" formulas, avoid fee-splitting and kickback-type structures. Federal anti-kickback law mainly matters for federal healthcare program payers, but many state laws are all-payer. Counsel to confirm.
- **Dietitian licensure.** Software that gives *individualized* nutrition or supplement advice based on a person's health conditions could be argued to be the "practice of dietetics" or MNT in strict states. Mitigations:
  - frame outputs as general nutrition and supplement information and product selection;
  - avoid disease-specific MNT language ("diet for your PCOS");
  - offer human 1:1 nutrition coaching only through licensed RDs or properly credentialed providers in each state;
  - geo-limit features in Alabama, Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota and Tennessee if counsel advises.
- **Using labs from other providers.** If users upload outside labs and Vilo's algorithm alone interprets them, there is no physician, so the device analysis (Section 3) and the practice-of-medicine or dietetics analysis apply most directly. A clinician-in-the-loop model reduces both risks but adds CPOM and HIPAA complexity.

- Dietitian licensure data comes mainly from advocacy groups and is current only to about late 2024 or 2025. State-by-state verification is needed.

### Takeaway

Personalized multi-ingredient formulas, women's-health positioning (pregnancy and fertility) and a data-heavy model justify adding recall and cyber/privacy coverage. ### Cited Findings
- Broker Insurance Canopy says most supplement businesses it insures pay $2,800-$4,500 per year, and higher-risk operations $10,000+ per year. Source: [Insurance Canopy](https://www.insurancecanopy.com/vitamins-and-dietary-supplements-insurance).
- Western Financial Group cites $1,000-$5,000+ per year for supplement stores. Source: [Western Financial Group](https://westernfinancialgroup.ca/What-Insurance-Do-Nutritional-Supplement-Stores-Need).
- General liability alone is about $350-$750 per year for $1M coverage, excluding product liability. Source: [TRUiC / HowToStartAnLLC, Feb 2026](https://howtostartanllc.com/business-insurance/business-insurance-for-supplement-businesses).
- Product recall insurance covers the costs of removing contaminated products and related PR. No premium data was given. Source: [Insureon](https://www.insureon.com/retail-business-insurance/vitamin-stores).
- Industry columnists note that insurance is "particularly onerous" for supplement startups, and that carriers' recall and Prop 65 coverage may be narrower than marketed. Sources: [Nutraceuticals World](https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/will-negativity-surrounding-supplements-raise-my-liability-insurance-costs/); [The Hartford, supplement insurance overview](https://www.thehartford.com/business-insurance/dietary-supplement-insurance).

### Inferences
- **Coverage stack to price:**
  - products/completed-operations liability, with limits that retailers and contract manufacturers often require ($1-5M);
  - vendor's endorsement from the contract manufacturer;
  - product recall and contamination;
  - cyber/privacy liability, specifically covering regulatory defense (FTC/AG), privacy class actions (CIPA/MHMDA wiretap claims are often excluded or sub-limited; review "wrongful collection" exclusions) and breach response;
  - D&O;
  - tech errors and omissions (E&O) covering recommendation-engine errors (for example over-dosing a user on vitamin A or iron);
  - if clinicians are involved, medical malpractice coverage held by the professional entity.
- **Engine-specific risks.** Personalization creates distinct product-liability theories: failure to screen for contraindications (pregnancy, anticoagulants, thyroid medication, SSRIs with certain botanicals) or for nutrient upper limits. Mitigations:
  - hard-coded safety rules (at or below the UL; pregnancy and lactation screens; drug-nutrient interaction checks);
  - pharmacist or clinician review of the rule set;
  - documented validation;
  - a clear warning label on each pack.

### Gaps
- No current (2025-2026) independent data on recall-insurance or cyber premiums for supplement or health-app startups. Get broker quotes.
- No data on how carriers price the privacy-litigation risk of health apps after Flo.

## 7. California Prop 65 (lead) and state restrictions on certain supplements (New York's ban on sales to minors)

### Takeaway
Prop 65 lead warnings are the most common state-law trap for supplements. The safe-harbor level is 0.5 µg of lead per day, calculated at the maximum labeled daily serving. Each unique personalized formula has its own exposure, so Vilo needs worst-case testing and a warning strategy. New York (since April 22, 2024) bans selling weight-loss and muscle-building supplements to under-18s, so Vilo should age-gate at 18+.

### Cited Findings

**Prop 65**
- The Prop 65 lead safe-harbor level is 0.5 µg per day. It applies uniformly to all products, supplements included, and consent judgments for supplements use this threshold. Sources: [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/environmental-law/publications/proposition-65-plaintiff-group-seeks-to-rescind-safe-harbor-standard-for-lead/); [CA AG 2025 settlement](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/prop65/settlements/2025-03514S7722.pdf).
- Daily exposure is calculated as lead per gram × grams per serving × servings per day, using the largest labeled serving size and daily servings (one serving if none is stated). Source: [CA AG 2025 settlement](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/prop65/settlements/2025-03514S7722.pdf).
- Mateel Environmental Justice Foundation challenged the lead safe harbor. A California Court of Appeal deferred to OEHHA and upheld it. Sources: [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/environmental-law/publications/proposition-65-plaintiff-group-seeks-to-rescind-safe-harbor-standard-for-lead/); [CalChamber](https://advocacy.calchamber.com/?p=30134).
- Enforcement is active. Sixty-day notices against supplements for lead continued through 2025 and into 2026 (for example notice 2025-03331, settled November 11, 2025). Sources: [CA AG 60-Day Notice 2025-03331](https://oag.ca.gov/prop65/60-Day-Notice-2025-03331); [Eurofins Prop 65 overview](https://www.eurofins.com/en-us/food-feed-testing/resource-library/proposition-65-in-food-dietary-supplements-key-risks-limits-and-compliance/).

**New York minors law**
- The law took effect April 22, 2024. It bans the sale of OTC diet pills and supplements marketed for weight loss or muscle building to people under 18, and requires proof of age.
- Coverage is judged by ingredients and marketing. Creatine, green tea extract, raspberry ketone, garcinia and green coffee bean are cited as possible triggers. Protein products are exempt unless they contain such ingredients.
- A retail platform categorizing a product under "weight loss" may bring it within the law. Guidance on online and delivery sales is lacking.
- CRN challenged the law as overly broad, and the outcome was not found.
- Sources: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/04/25/new-york-outlaws-sales-of-diet-pills-muscle-mass-builders-children/); [Venable, Nov 2023](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2023/11/new-york-state-prohibits-sales-of-diet-pills-and); [Kelley Drye snapshot](https://s3.amazonaws.com/cdn.kelleydrye.com/content/uploads/pdf-snapshots/new-york-weight-loss-supplement-law-has-stakeholders-scrambling-but-faces-legal-challenges-20250929170449.pdf).
- 2024 reporting said Massachusetts and California were considering similar bills (an earlier California version was vetoed). Source: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/04/25/new-york-outlaws-sales-of-diet-pills-muscle-mass-builders-children/).

### Inferences
- **Prop 65 for personalized formulas:**
  - Model lead exposure per *formula* by summing each ingredient's lead contribution at the actual dose, using certificate-of-analysis (CoA) heavy-metals data, and verify by testing finished packs.
  - Either cap total daily lead below 0.5 µg in the formulation engine (best) or apply a compliant warning.
  - Note that OEHHA's "short-form" warning rules were amended (effective 2025, according to the researcher's background knowledge, not verified this session).
  - Minerals (iron, zinc, calcium, magnesium) and botanicals are the usual lead sources.
  - Online sales to California need the warning before purchase.
- **Age gating.** Vilo should be 18+. Avoid weight-loss and "metabolism" positioning and ingredients like green tea extract unless age-gated. Watch for similar laws in other states.

### Gaps
- Current status of the CRN lawsuit against New York's law and any 2025-2026 copycat state laws: not found. The Fortune article also reported a Colorado law (effective July 2024), which was not verified.
- OEHHA's 2024-2025 amendments to short-form warnings: not verified this session.

## 8. HSA/FSA eligibility (letters of medical necessity, Truemed, and the IRS stance)

### Takeaway
Supplements are generally not qualified medical expenses. They qualify only "rarely", when used to treat or prevent a specific diagnosed medical condition and documented by a genuine letter of medical necessity (LMN). The IRS warned in 2024 that questionnaire-based LMNs from companies marketing to consumers are inadequate. Using a Truemed-type LMN workflow for Vilo carries audit risk for customers and false-advertising and regulatory risk for Vilo. It would also undercut Vilo's "wellness, not disease" positioning, because an LMN requires a disease purpose.

### Cited Findings
- The IRS warned in 2024 that some companies wrongly claim doctor's letters based only on self-reported information can make non-medical items (gym memberships, dietary supplements) eligible. An IRS spokesperson said food and supplements are only "rarely" medical expenses, under strict circumstances. Sources: [Kiplinger](https://kiplinger.com/taxes/hsa-expenses-when-a-doctors-note-isnt-enough); [Yahoo/WSJ syndication](https://www.yahoo.com/news/why-irs-doesn-t-believe-015727575.html); [HealthEquity](https://www.healthequity.com/blog/irs-reminds-taxpayers-ineligible-expenses); [DBS Benefits](https://www.dbsbenefits.com/irs-alert-pretax-funds-arent-for-health-and-wellness/).
- Truemed's model: the consumer completes an online health form, a remote clinician reviews it, and an LMN is issued if the product is needed to treat or prevent a condition (for example diabetes, heart disease or obesity). It launched for supplements in October 2024. Sources: [NutraIngredients-USA, Oct 2024](https://nutraingredients-usa.com/Article/2024/10/22/Truemed-unlocks-HSA-FSA-coverage-for-dietary-supplements); [Truemed blog, company source](https://www.truemed.com/blog/the-hsa-market-were-building/).
- Former IRS Commissioner Danny Werfel argued, in a Bloomberg Tax opinion piece, that the IRS should clarify that telehealth can support LMNs and that eligibility should depend on purpose, not product category. He noted the 2025 tax package permanently extended the HSA telehealth safe harbor. No new IRS guidance was found. Source: [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-tax-report/irs-reset-on-health-savings-accounts-would-boost-maha-outcomes).

### Inferences
- **Do not** advertise "HSA/FSA eligible" for Vilo formulas in general. At most say "may be eligible with a letter of medical necessity; consult your plan". Overstating eligibility is a deceptive claim and could draw FTC or state AG interest.
- An LMN requires a disease purpose, which collides with structure/function positioning. Disease-purpose LMNs plus disease-framed app copy could be used as evidence of drug intended use.
- If Vilo partners with an LMN provider, the clinician must make a genuine individualized determination. Vilo should not script outcomes and should keep the partner's data flows HIPAA/BAA-compliant.

### Gaps
- The exact IRS release number and date for the 2024 warning were not verified this session (background knowledge: IRS news release, March 2024). Any 2025-2026 IRS action on LMN companies was not found.

## 9. EU and UK differences (summary)

### Takeaway
The EU and UK are materially stricter on claims and data.
- **Claims.** Only EFSA-authorized health claims (EU Register) or GB-authorized claims may be used. There is no US-style structure/function freedom.
- **Market entry.** Many EU member states require pre-market notification (Germany BVL, Italy NSIS, France Teleicare), and national rules on maximum vitamin and mineral levels and botanicals diverge because EU-wide maximum levels were never set.
- **Data.** Health data is GDPR Article 9 special-category data, so personalized nutrition generally needs *explicit consent*. The CJEU reads "health data" broadly.

Per-customer formulas create an extra hurdle where national notification is per product. Whether each personalized combination needs its own notification is a key open question.

### Cited Findings
- **Directive 2002/46/EC (Food Supplements):**
  - it harmonizes permitted vitamin and mineral forms and labeling;
  - Article 10 lets, but does not require, member states to demand notification before marketing;
  - maximum and minimum amounts were to be set at EU level but have never been adopted, so national limits apply.
  - Sources: [Directive 2002/46/EC, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ%3AL%3A2002%3A183%3A0051%3A0057%3AEN%3APDF); [European Commission discussion paper on max/min levels](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b13c2e05-0f5e-44a0-8d7b-aadc1613759e_en?filename=labelling_nutrition-supplements-discus_paper_amount_vitamins_en.pdf); [Freyr, secondary](https://www.freyrsolutions.com/food-supplements/food-and-food-supplements-registration-in-the-european-union).
- **National notification:**
  - Germany: online notification to the BVL at the latest when first marketed in Germany, including for products already notified elsewhere in the EU.
  - Italy: notification to the Ministry of Health via NSIS, which publishes a list of notified products.
  - France: notification through DGCCRF's Teleicare, often followed by review and objections.
  - Botanicals are treated differently: Italy uses the BELFRIT list, while France is more restrictive.
  - Health claims and formulations used in one country are not automatically acceptable across the EU.
  - Sources: [German federal services portal (BVL notification)](https://verwaltung.bund.de/leistungsverzeichnis/en/leistung/99118031261000); [BfR guidance](https://www.bfr.bund.de/cm/349/distribution_and_import_of_foods_including_food_supplements.pdf); [NutraIngredients, May 19 2026](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/05/19/european-expansion-through-replication-the-costly-regulatory-pitfall/); [ARWS](https://arws.cz/news-at-arrows/eu-food-supplement-compliance); [French government food-supplements note](https://entreprises.gouv.fr/files/directions_services/free-movement-goods/food-supplements.pdf).
- **Claims.** Under the Nutrition and Health Claims Regulation (EC 1924/2006), health claims must be scientifically substantiated and authorized after EFSA assessment. Novel Food and Medical Device Regulation overlaps are also flagged for personalized nutrition. Source: [Leatherhead Food Research](https://www.leatherheadfood.com/news-detail/blog-regulations-impact-personalised-nutrition/).
- **No dedicated EU regime.** The EU has no sui generis regulation for personalized nutrition; it is governed by fragmented food, claims, data and device law (2020 analysis). Source: [Cambridge, European Journal of Risk Regulation](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-journal-of-risk-regulation/article/personalised-nutrition-the-eus-fragmented-legal-landscape-and-the-overlooked-implications-of-eu-food-law/B285FA84E4E7EC5C28F7DF83928F22C5).
- **GDPR:**
  - For personalized nutrition based on health data, explicit consent is generally the only available Article 9 lawful basis.
  - "Health data" is interpreted broadly, including glucose levels, test results and BMI.
  - The CJEU has held that data processed by e-commerce operators selling health-related products can be health data.
  - Sources: [Vitafoods Insights](https://www.vitafoodsinsights.com/personalised-nutrition/what-does-gdpr-mean-for-personalised-nutrition-); [Portolano Cavallo](https://portolano.it/en/blog/life-sciences/do-users-data-processed-ecommerce-operators-qualify-health-data-ecj-says-yes-).
  - The European Health Data Space Regulation adds a further layer for health data processing. Source: [Aumans Avocats](https://aumans-avocats.com/en/gdpr-and-ehds-regulation-on-health-data-of-the-european-space-ehds-which-rules-for-the-processing-of-health-data-in-europe/).
- **UK:**
  - Retained Directive structure via the Food Supplements (England) Regulations 2003 and devolved equivalents. After Brexit, the vitamin and mineral lists were moved into the Nutrition (Amendment etc.) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019.
  - **No statutory UK maximum levels.** The voluntary guideline levels from the 2003 Expert Group on Vitamins and Minerals (EVM) apply.
  - Sources: [GOV.UK guidance notes](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/food-supplements-guidance-and-faqs/guidance-notes-on-legislation-implementing-directive-200246ec-on-food-supplements); [House of Commons Library note](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/SN04152/SN04152.pdf).
- **UK MHRA borderline products:**
  - The MHRA treats food supplements as potentially "borderline" medicines and decides case by case.
  - Products with familiar vitamins and minerals are generally regulated as food.
  - Medicinal claims (per MHRA Guidance Note 8, updated August 2026) can make a product a medicine.
  - The Medicines Borderline Advice Form gives pre-market opinions.
  - Sources: [GOV.UK borderline products](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/borderline-products-how-to-tell-if-your-product-is-a-medicine); [MHRA GN8, Aug 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a747082bde92a23046b246e/GN8_FINAL_20260806.pdf); [NHS SPS](https://www.sps.nhs.uk/articles/understanding-food-supplements/).

### Inferences
- **Claims.** US copy cannot be reused. Only authorized wording applies, for example "Iron contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue" and "Vitamin B6 contributes to the regulation of hormonal activity", each with its conditions of use. Claims such as "personalized to balance your hormones" are unauthorized health claims or medicinal claims.
- **Notification.** Per-user formulas may need notification of every combination in notification states. Firms usually notify a defined set of "modules" or SKUs combined in a pack. Counsel to confirm per country.
- **Dose limits.** National maximum-level divergence (for example Germany's BfR recommended maximums and Italy's ministerial limits) constrains the dosing engine per country. A per-market rules table is needed.
- **Data.** Explicit consent, a data protection impact assessment (DPIA), and possibly a DPO and EU representative. Restricted international transfers mean US cloud hosting needs the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses (SCCs).
- **Software.** The recommendation engine could be a medical device under EU MDR Rule 11 if it gives information used for diagnostic or therapeutic decisions. MDR is stricter than the US 2026 wellness guidance. Counsel to confirm.
- **UK.** Lighter on maximum levels, with no notification requirement for supplements in GB according to the researcher's background knowledge (not verified). It still has an authorized-claims-only regime (the GB NHC Register), UK GDPR special-category rules, and MHRA scrutiny of medicinal claims.

### Gaps
- Not verified: the GB Nutrition and Health Claims Register specifics, UK notification status (believed none in GB), EU MDR Rule 11 application to nutrition apps, and the EU AI Act's possible application to health-related recommendation systems.
- Official Italian and French notification procedure texts were not fetched.
- No source found on any EU or UK regulator action specifically against personalized supplement packs.

## 10. Enforcement examples relevant to personalized nutrition, women's health and supplement subscriptions

### Takeaway
The most relevant precedents:
- the FTC's GeneLink/foru case (personalized supplements: unsupported personalization claims plus data security);
- the FTC privacy cases against health and fertility apps (Flo 2021, Premom 2023, GoodRx 2023, BetterHelp 2023);
- the private CIPA verdict against Meta in the Flo litigation (2025);
- FDA warning letters treating fertility and PCOS claims as drug claims;
- the FTC's 2026 supplement actions (Amare, TruHeight) and review-rule warning letters (December 2025);
- ROSCA enforcement against subscription and telehealth sellers (NextMed).

Sources: [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/08/ftc-updated-health-breach-notification-rule); [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/zh/insights/publications/2024/7/ftc-updates-health-breach-notification-rule).

## 11. Synthesis: highest-risk items and do/don't guidance for Vilo

### Takeaway
The five highest legal risks, in rough order:
1. **Ad-tech or third-party sharing of health and cycle data.** Exposure under CIPA, MHMDA and HBNR, with class-action scale damages (Flo, GoodRx).
2. **Disease claims.** PCOS, fertility, menopause, anemia or thyroid claims, made directly, through testimonials or in algorithm outputs, which turn the product into an unapproved drug and the software into a device.
3. **A patient-facing lab-interpretation engine** that diagnoses "deficiencies" and doses "treatment". Device risk, plus practice-of-medicine and dietetics risk.
4. **Unsubstantiated "personalization works" and "clinically formulated" claims** (GeneLink precedent; FTC RCT expectation).
5. **Subscription mechanics** (ROSCA; California's AB 2863 auto-renewal amendments).

Material but more manageable: Prop 65 lead per formula, label compliance for variable packs, adverse-event reporting, age-gating, and HSA/FSA claims.

### Cited Findings
- See the citations in Sections 1-10. Key anchors:
  - [FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Health-Products-Compliance-Guidance.pdf)
  - [HBNR Final Rule (Federal Register)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/30/2024-10855/health-breach-notification-rule)
  - [Flo verdict (Fierce Healthcare)](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/meta-found-liable-abusing-consumer-reproductive-health-data-run-targeted-ads)
  - [FDA 2026 wellness/CDS (Arnold & Porter)](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/01/fda-cuts-red-tape-on-clinical-decision-support-software)
  - [FDC Nutrition warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/fdc-nutrition-inc-612264-02182021)
  - [GeneLink (FTC)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2014/01/companies-pitching-genetically-customized-nutritional-supplements)
  - [CA AB 2863 (Barnes & Thornburg)](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect)
  - [Maryland MODPA (Manatt)](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/privacy-and-data-security/now-in-effect-maryland-law-raises-bar-on-sensitive-data-data-minimization-and-children-s-privacy)

### Inferences

**DO**
- **Claims governance**
  - Maintain a fixed, counsel-approved structure/function claim library with substantiation files.
  - File the 30-day FDA notice for those claims.
  - Show the DSHEA disclaimer, with asterisk linkage, on labels, the website and in-app claim screens.
- **Safe engine design**
  - Frame recommendations as wellness and nutrient-intake support.
  - Cap doses at or below the UL.
  - Hard-code contraindication screens: pregnancy and lactation, anticoagulants, thyroid medication, SSRIs, hormonal contraceptives with certain botanicals, kidney disease.
  - Route out-of-range lab values to "consider discussing with your clinician" messages without naming conditions (consistent with the 2026 General Wellness guidance).
- **Clinician model (if used)**
  - Put any lab ordering and clinician review in a CPOM-compliant professional entity, with a BAA and segregated PHI.
  - Make the clinician-facing tool meet all four CDS criteria.
- **Labels for every personalized pack**
  - Variable-data Supplement Facts reflecting the exact formula, plus the ingredient list, distributor and AER contact, lot code and expiry.
  - Verify with counsel how the multi-unit exemption applies to sachets.
- **Privacy**
  - Separate opt-in consents.
  - A Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy (required by Washington and Nevada).
  - No sale of health data.
  - Minimize data and keep cycle data on-device or end-to-end encrypted.
  - Server-side analytics with no ad pixels or SDKs on health surfaces.
  - A law-enforcement request policy and a transparency report.
  - An HBNR breach-response plan.
  - Build to NY HIPA and Maryland MODPA now.
- **Subscriptions**
  - Meet California AB 2863 and ROSCA: clear disclosures, separate consent, online one-click cancellation, and renewal and price-change reminders.
- **Prop 65**
  - Model lead per formula and keep daily lead below 0.5 µg where feasible; otherwise show a compliant warning before purchase.
- **Operations**
  - Set up a serious adverse event intake and reporting SOP (15 business days).
  - Register any facility Vilo itself operates.
  - Age-gate at 18+.

**DON'T**
- Don't claim to treat, prevent or "support" a named condition: PCOS, infertility, endometriosis, PMDD, anemia, hypothyroidism, osteoporosis, depression, or "menopause treatment". This includes testimonials, influencer posts, SEO pages and app outputs.
- Don't make conception or contraception claims from cycle tracking.
- Don't present "clinically proven", "doctor-designed" or "personalization is more effective" without RCT-level support and genuine expert involvement.
- Don't share health or cycle data with ad platforms, data brokers or "lookalike" audiences, or build marketing inferences from health inputs.
- Don't use peptides, hormone precursors, or contested and drug-precluded ingredients. Don't rely on the deregulatory political climate for ingredient status.
- Don't market "HSA/FSA eligible" for general formulas.
- Don't incentivize positive reviews or suppress negative ones.
- Don't make cancellation harder than sign-up.
- Don't launch in the EU or UK with US copy or US dosing. EU/UK need authorized claims only, national notification and maximum levels, and GDPR Article 9 explicit consent.

### Gaps
- All of the above needs confirmation by FDA/FTC regulatory counsel and privacy counsel, especially:
  - the multi-unit label exemption for sachets;
  - CDS and wellness treatment of consumer lab interpretation in the 2026 guidance texts;
  - whether a "deficiency" message falls under §403(r)(6)(A);
  - state dietitian-licensure exposure;
  - NY HIPA's final status.
- Primary documents could not be fetched in this session (fda.gov, ftc.gov and several law-firm sites were blocked), and the web-search budget was exhausted. A follow-up pass with network access should verify the items flagged as "background knowledge" above.
